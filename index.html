EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 10, 2026

Two labor markets, one continent, opposite trajectories. The US added 115,000 jobs in April, almost double consensus. Canada lost 17,700 — and full-time jobs cratered by 46,700, dragging the unemployment rate to a six-month high of 6.9%. YTD, Canada has shed roughly 112,000 jobs, the worst four-month stretch since the pandemic. Carney's "decoupling from the US" thesis — the one I leaned on after the April 19 SCIP speech — is fracturing in real time. The goods-producing sector, the part of Canada's economy directly exposed to Trump's tariff regime, lost 26,800 jobs in a single month. You cannot decouple from the dollar that buys 75% of your exports.

Meanwhile, Friday revealed the second act of the Intel playbook nobody is naming yet. The federal government took 9.9% of Intel last August. This week the WSJ broke that Apple has signed a preliminary deal for Intel to fab future chips — and Trump personally lobbied Tim Cook in a White House meeting to make it happen. Intel +13.96% in one session. This is not chip news. This is a 9-month industrial-policy template: federal equity stake → presidential customer steering → AAA private-sector commitment. Wolfspeed, Micron, Onsemi, GlobalFoundries are next in the queue. If your portfolio touches semis, this is the most predictive macro pattern of 2026.

The bridge for me — operating in both India and Canada — is that sovereign-state behavior is now the dominant variable in business outcomes, more than rates, more than commodities. Tariffs gut Canada's full-time labor base. Federal equity creates US national champions. India's banks face a frontier-AI cyber shock (PNB just earmarked ₹700–800 Cr — over 20% of its tech budget — for cyber, +50% YoY). Capital markets are pricing this slowly; jobs reports and chip deals are pricing it instantly. I think the next 90 days reward whoever maps "which company does the state want to win" more aggressively than "which company has the best fundamentals."

SEMIS · INDUSTRIAL POLICY▲3-12 month implicationsACT
Trump personally pitched Cook on Intel — and Apple just blinked. The federal-equity playbook now has its second confirmation.

Apple and Intel have signed a preliminary chip-manufacturing agreement, per the WSJ on Friday. Talks ran for over a year; Trump personally advocated for Intel during a White House meeting with Cook, per people familiar. Intel closed at $124.92, up 13.96% on Friday — its single best session of 2026. Apple shares were up roughly 1.7%. Production is reportedly slated for as early as 2027 on M-class chips for Macs/iPads, ending Apple's near-total TSMC dependency for new silicon.

SO WHAT in India: Indian semi-fab ambitions (Tata Electronics-PSMC at Dholera, Micron Sanand) just got a competitive read — the federal-equity playbook is what gives Intel pricing power Apple was forced to accept. India's PLI subsidies don't equal a 9.9% Treasury equity stake. Indian fabs need to package preferential customer steering, not just capex match-funding. SO WHAT in Canada: Canada has no comparable national-champion model and no fab anchor. Carney's industrial policy framework — which I read as "supplier of clean energy and critical minerals" — looks reactive, not coercive. If you operate Canadian-side businesses in tech-adjacent supply chains, you do not have a Trump-with-Cook-shaped catalyst behind you. Plan accordingly.
ACTION: Map every public US semi for two attributes: (1) US fab footprint and strategic node, (2) lack of existing Big Tech anchor commitment. Top of the screen: Wolfspeed, Micron, GlobalFoundries, Onsemi. Watch Treasury filings, CHIPS-Act amendment news, and any presidential-level meeting readouts within 60-120 days. The 9-month Intel template tells you when, not if.
💭 The version where this is wrong: Apple-Intel is a face-saving "preliminary" handshake that never converts to volume. Intel's process node is 18A — a generation behind TSMC's 2nm. Apple may have committed to lower-margin, older chips (server-class M-series for cloud, not iPhone). If yields disappoint by Q3 2026, Cook walks. The federal-equity-to-customer pattern is then a one-off, not a template, and chasing Wolfspeed/Micron on that basis is portfolio cosplay.

The Long Game
Sam Altman quietly buried his three-year UBI experiment this week — the study found that direct cash transfers increased spending but didn't improve health or healthcare access. His new proposal: "stake in AI's upside" through compute access, equity, or a public wealth fund. Think Alaska Permanent Fund, but for compute. The Moonshots crew called this "universal basic compute" and noted that GPT-5.5 Instant is already free for hundreds of millions. Set aside whether you find the moral arithmetic compelling — the political arithmetic is now real. India operates the world's largest cash-transfer welfare apparatus (DBT, ~$300B disbursed in a decade); Canada is debating the Carney-era social-spending floor. Universal basic compute is a phrase to mark in the index now, because by 2027 some serious country (probably India, possibly Singapore, less likely Canada) is going to issue a sovereign-compute-credit pilot. The argument that compute is a public good that should be allocated like roads and ports — not bought like a SaaS subscription — will arrive faster than the ChatGPT moment did.
