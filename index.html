EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 14, 2026 · Evening

The AI capital flywheel hit a tick-resolution today that should make every operator stop and look. Cerebras priced at $185 last night, opened at $350 this morning, and traded above $370 inside the first hour. That is not a normal IPO pop; that is the public market telling underwriters they mispriced AI-compute by close to ninety per cent in a single tick. On the same morning, Anthropic is in talks to raise $30B at a $900B valuation — ahead of OpenAI's $852B from March, with an annualised revenue run rate the company now puts at $30B and on track to $50B by June per WSJ. And Ramp's data shows Anthropic crossed OpenAI in business adoption (34.4% vs 32.3%) inside twelve months from a 9% base last May.

Here is the piece almost nobody is naming out loud except Robin Wigglesworth in the FT and Adam Tooze quoting him: the same hyperscalers funding Anthropic and OpenAI are booking the markup on those equity stakes as "other income". Alphabet logged $37.7B of it in Q1 — more than half of net income. Amazon ran $16B versus $2.7B in the prior comparable quarter. Across the top-five hyperscalers, Goldman counts $53B of "other income" in Q1, of which $49B was explicitly equity-stake markups. That is 34% of all top-5 hyperscaler Q1 income generated by valuing private holdings that the hyperscalers themselves are the largest funders of — and the AI labs are turning that capital back into cloud commitments that now make up roughly half of the order books at Oracle / Alphabet / Amazon / Microsoft. The capital is going in a circle and the public markets just signed off on the next leg of it with Cerebras.

The India-Canada cross-read: USD/INR 95.92 today, fresh record territory; Sensex +0.92% recovering on the gold-duty defence narrative; WTI $100.87 with Iran rejecting the MoU and the ceasefire on what Trump publicly called "life support". Meanwhile in Canada, Ottawa and Alberta are within hours of an industrial-carbon-pricing-for-pipeline MoU that softens the price escalation from $170/t-by-2030 all the way to $130/t-by-2040 — a structural climate-policy capitulation in exchange for a Pacific bitumen pipeline plus Pathways carbon-capture. The cross-currency picture diverges sharply: India is legislating capital controls (gold duty hike, oil-shock pass-through) while Canada is legislating capital deployment (industrial price softened, pipeline cleared, Nouveau Monde graphite financed at $645M with a Government-of-Canada offtake). One sovereign is defending its currency; the other is monetising its rocks.

Forward call: I think Cerebras gives back at least 15% of the open by Friday close as profit-taking and SemiAnalysis's wafer-scale architectural critique catch up with the float — that is P174-1 below. And I think Carney and Smith stand on a podium in Calgary or Edmonton inside the next seven days; the cabinet leak today is the political tell, not the policy. The slower carbon price is the cost of buying domestic political peace for an export-capital decade.

Per Globe and Mail / CBC / CTV sources, Ottawa and Alberta are finalising a memorandum of understanding that raises the provincial industrial carbon price from $95/tonne today to $130/tonne by 2040 — a slower escalation versus the federal-schedule target of $170/tonne by 2030. The accumulating signal is a national-prosperity-first decade in which environmental policy is the bargaining chip and infrastructure is the deliverable. The implicit thesis: Canada as the export-grade physical-layer supplier for North American AI-infra, oil-and-gas, and critical minerals.
