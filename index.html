EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · June 12, 2026

For a few hours last night the whole world was held hostage to a single social-media post. Trump warned he would hit Iran "very hard," then reversed within the evening, cancelled the strike, and announced a "great settlement" he says could be signed this weekend — "maybe in Europe." Equities ripped (S&P +1.7%, Nasdaq +2.5%, Dow +900), oil fell 3.6% to $86.83. Then Tehran said there is "no final decision." I have watched this exact sequence play out repeatedly since April: imminent-deal, collapse, re-escalation, imminent-deal. I do not believe the weekend signing until I see signatures. The tradeable fact is not the peace — it is the volatility: one man's post is now worth a 4% move in crude.

Here is the connection nobody is drawing cleanly. The same week Trump dangles peace on Iran, he says he is "not looking to renew" USMCA and that the US doesn't "need anything" Canada and Mexico have — and a US strike on a Gulf of Oman tanker killed three Indian sailors. Both of my home markets are being shown the same lesson from opposite ends: India absorbs dead citizens and stays quiet because it is racing to close a trade deal; Canada gets told its trade pact may not be renewed at all. The loonie sits at 1.397, the rupee near a record 95.3. These are not two stories. They are one — the price of being a mid-power in a world where Washington has decided leverage beats alliance.

And underneath it all, the macro has quietly flipped. The ECB hiked for the first time since 2023; the World Bank cut global growth to 2.5%, the weakest since the pandemic. The era of synchronized easing is over. My call: watch whether oil holds last night's drop into Monday. If Iran's "no final decision" hardens, the $86 print reverses, the ECB's hike looks early, and the relief rally becomes the bull trap of the quarter.

MARKETS · EUROPE↑quartersWATCH
ECB hikes for the first time since 2023 — the easing era is officially over and the Fed is next in the dock

The ECB raised its deposit rate to 2.25% and refi rate to 2.40% on Thursday — its first hike in nearly three years — citing "a broadening of inflation throughout the economy." Eurozone inflation hit 3.2% in May (highest since Sept 2023) on a 10.9% energy surge; Frankfurt lifted its 2026 inflation forecast to ~3.0% and trimmed GDP growth to ~0.8%. Markets now price two or more further hikes this year. The ECB is the first major developed central bank to hike since the Iran war began — and a US inflation spike (May CPI 4.2%) is now pressuring the Fed and its new chair before the June 16–17 FOMC.

SO WHAT: The global rate regime has flipped from "cut" to "hike/hold," and that re-rates everything leveraged. For India, a hawkish Fed plus weak rupee compounds imported-inflation and FII-outflow pressure. For Canada, the BoC is now boxed: it can't follow the ECB up without choking a slowing economy, but can't cut into oil-driven inflation either (see Canadian File).
ACTION: Re-price duration assumptions. The June FOMC (Jun 16–17) is now the key event — a hold-or-hike confirms the regime shift; watch the 10Y UST testing 4.60%.
💭 The dovish case: this is a one-and-done energy-spike hike. If Iran genuinely settles and oil holds $86, the inflation impulse fades fast and the ECB pauses in July — making this hike the cycle's last, not its first.

THE LONG GAME

The quiet structural story of this week isn't any single headline — it's the regime change underneath all of them. For three years the entire developed world ran one playbook: inflation falling, central banks cutting, capital cheapening. That playbook just inverted. The ECB hiked for the first time since 2023, US CPI is back at 4.2%, the Fed is being pushed toward a hike, and the World Bank cut global growth to its slowest since the pandemic. The cause is a single exogenous shock — an energy war routed through Hormuz — layered onto economies that never fully normalized. What makes it dangerous is that it's stagflationary: growth down and inflation up at the same time, which leaves every central bank with the BoC's "conundrum" and no clean tool. For operators carrying leverage, the lesson of the next several quarters is that the cost of capital is going the wrong way precisely as growth softens — the opposite of the environment most balance sheets were built for. The single Trump post that erased 4% of oil last night is a preview of how violently this regime will trade: thin liquidity, headline-driven, two-sided. Plan for the volatility, not the direction.
