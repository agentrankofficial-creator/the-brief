Suhail Seth · May 21, 2026

The center of gravity changed yesterday and almost nobody named it correctly. Three supertankers crossed Hormuz. One South Korean VLCC, the Universal Winner, hauling two million barrels of Kuwaiti crude that had been waiting since March 4, followed two Chinese tankers out. It was the first significant transit since May 4. Trump said the US is in the "final stages" of an Iran peace deal. WTI fell 5.45% to $98.47 in a single session. The dollar weakened slightly. The rupee strengthened from 96.43 to 96.73. The petrodollar-unwind narrative I spent yesterday's edition unpacking did not reverse — but the proximate forcing function has shifted regime.

The second half of the same picture: Putin and Xi sat in Beijing, signed forty documents and a 47-page joint declaration on a "multipolar world order" — and could not put a number on Power of Siberia 2. Peskov used the phrase "basic understanding" and "no clear timeline" — almost exactly the soft language I predicted in P180-3 yesterday. The Russia-China strategic counter-move to a reopening Hormuz is stuck on price. Russia needs the volume; China is happy to let Russia wait. That is a buyer's market for hydrocarbons, two days after Hormuz signalled it might reopen.

Cross-reading both: if Iran de-escalates in the next 30 days, every macro position taken in the last four weeks gets re-priced. The Indian importer story is the obvious one — USD/INR walks back from 96.7 toward 95, gold-duty hike messaging looks like overshoot, Modi's gold-curb pivot needs softer language. The Canadian story is the non-obvious one — a sustained Hormuz reopening weakens the petro-CAD-supportive bid, and the sovereign critical-minerals re-rating I was leaning into in #180 has to be re-tested against a world where chokepoint risk is no longer the price-setter. The cleaner Canadian long this morning is the Cohere/Reliant biopharma deal: Canada doubling down on vertical-AI consolidation while the US frontier labs eat each other's senior talent (Karpathy from OpenAI to Anthropic to lead "training Claude to accelerate Claude" — read that sentence twice). My forward call: WTI tests $90 before June 30. If that hits, the rupee is back below 95 by mid-July, and the Canadian story stops being about chokepoints and goes back to being about productivity.

GEOPOLITICS↓ Oil30–60 daysACT
Three tankers cross Hormuz, WTI collapses 5.45% to $98.47, Trump says US is in "final stages" of Iran peace — the chokepoint that drove three months of macro just started to unjam, while in Beijing Putin and Xi failed to put a price on Power of Siberia 2

The South Korean VLCC Universal Winner, carrying 2 million barrels of Kuwaiti crude loaded on March 4, transited the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday — following two Chinese tankers that exited earlier the same day. It was the first material transit since May 4, which had held shipping at ~5% of pre-conflict levels for the better part of three weeks. WTI dropped from $102.39 (Tuesday close) to $98.47 in a single session, down 5.45%. President Trump told reporters the US is in the "final stages" of a peace deal; Project Freedom remains paused. The Universal Winner is bound for Ulsan to discharge at SK Energy on June 9.

Same day, in Beijing: Putin and Xi extended the 2001 Treaty of Good-Neighborliness, signed 40 documents and a 47-page joint statement on a "multipolar world order" — and could not put a number on Power of Siberia 2. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov used the exact register I anticipated in P180-3: "basic understanding" on the route (through Mongolia, 50 bcm/year from Yamal to northern China) but "no clear timeline." Russia needed this deal far more than China needed it. China just told Moscow: we are happy to wait.

SO WHAT: The macro re-pricing chain is mechanical from here. If Hormuz transit normalises in the next 30 days, every long-USD/short-EM-importer position taken in the April-May window unwinds — INR, BRL, ZAR, IDR all firmer; 10Y UST term-premium compression accelerates; Brent term structure flattens. For Indian importers (oil, electronics, premium discretionary) the cash-flow forecasts written under the assumption of USD/INR 97-98 are now stale. For Canadian energy producers, a $90 WTI is a margin call on Q3 capex programmes that were re-written six weeks ago at $105. The cleaner second-order trade: the Russia stranded-gas problem widens, which means Asian LNG prices catch a bid through 2026 — supportive for Canadian LNG export economics even as crude weakens.
ACTION: Three falsifiable triggers to watch in the next 14 days. (1) Daily Hormuz transit count — ≥10 tankers/day average by June 4 confirms reopening regime; below 5 means today was a one-off. (2) Iran formal statement on Strait management; anything other than "graduated reopening with our concurrence" reads as posturing. (3) IRGC actions — any boarding or RPG fire on a transiting tanker resets the clock to $105+ crude. For operators with USD-cost exposure (Indian importers, North American refiners), do not hedge incremental Q3 needs at current strip until after the June OPEC+ meeting.
💭 The counter: this is the third "Hormuz reopening" headline since March — May 6 ceasefire pause was followed by IRGC boardings within 9 days; April 22 MSC Francesca incident wiped out the April 19 transit optimism in 72 hours. Iran has every incentive to maintain coercive control even after a nominal peace; the three tankers may be an inspection-pass exception negotiated for specific cargoes, not a reopening signal. The cleaner read may be: Iran extracted the price increase, three high-value cargoes got through, and the chokepoint resumes by June 1.

The mechanical chain: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Iraq, Iran combined account for ~30% of seaborne crude and ~25% of LNG. A reopened Hormuz at >75% throughput collapses the war risk premium that was running ~$15-18 of WTI's $102 print. The headline shock is the easy part. The non-obvious shock is term-premium compression on USTs — the foreign holders dumping in March (the petrodollar-unwind story I led with yesterday) had a feedback loop with oil, because Gulf reserves are the marginal buyer of dollar duration. If oil prints $90, Saudi/UAE reserves stop drawing down at the May pace, and the marginal demand for dollar duration improves at the margin. That is consistent with 10Y UST −9bp yesterday.

The Russia angle: Power of Siberia 2 was always a buyers-market negotiation. Russia has stranded gas (Yamal could supply 200+ bcm/year; pre-war Europe took ~150 bcm; post-war demand is structural-zero). China already has Power of Siberia 1 (38 bcm/year) and Power of Siberia 3 from Sakhalin (10 bcm). The only leverage Russia had was Mongolia routing speed — and Mongolia plays both sides. Result: China gets to wait, Russia gets the photo opportunity.

The India angle: A reopened Hormuz at any level above current 5% is unambiguously bullish for Indian importer margins. The May 13 gold/silver duty hike (6% → 15%) looks like overshoot if oil stays sub-$95; expect the duty to be quietly walked back within 60 days. RBI intervention scale visible in the May 28 weekly reserves release will be the key signal.

— THE LONG GAME —

The trend that will look obvious in late 2027 and is barely priced today: token throughput as a national-economy GDP metric. Google now processes 3.2 quadrillion tokens per month, up from 9.7 trillion two years ago — a 330x increase that maps almost cleanly to the productivity-output curve any classical economist would draw if a single technology delivered a 100x cost reduction in a primary input to white-collar work. For Indian and Canadian policy makers, the practical implication is that "AI infrastructure capex as % of GDP" is going to become a comparable cross-country metric within 18 months, the way "broadband penetration" became one in 2007-2010. The countries that move first on disclosing the metric — not just funding it — will get a regulatory-credibility premium with global capital, which is the actual currency that decides where the next 500MW of compute lands. Canada is closer to ready than India on this. Both are behind the UAE and Singapore. The 2027 narrative will be: nations are now ranked by the same metric as hyperscalers.
