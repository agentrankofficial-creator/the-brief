Editor's Read
Suhail Seth · May 9, 2026

The three loudest stories of the week — Anthropic crossing a $30B annualised run-rate after an 80-fold Q1, Cloudflare firing 1,100 people (20% of its workforce) and calling it an "agentic AI-first" reset, and another exchange of fire between US destroyers and Iranian boats inside the Strait of Hormuz — are not three stories. They are one. Compute is being priced as the new oil. Labor is being priced as the old oil. And Hormuz is reminding everyone that the actual oil still moves the tape.

Look at the chain. Anthropic plans for 10x growth, gets 80x, and within a week is renting Elon Musk's Colossus 1 — 300MW, 220,000 Nvidia GPUs — because it cannot self-build fast enough. Cloudflare, with revenue still up 34% YoY, decides 1,100 jobs are now "obsolete" because internal AI usage has 6x'd in 90 days. The market promptly wiped 24% off Cloudflare's cap, which is the part nobody is reading right: the Street is not yet rewarding "AI-first restructuring" — it is pricing it as a margin-pressure tell. That gap closes one of two ways. Either the next four S&P 500 layoff announcements deliver a guidance lift that proves Cloudflare was a leading edge, or the "tokens-as-labor" thesis becomes the new "metaverse pivot": a CFO talking-point that the buy-side has stopped paying for.

The Hormuz part of the trade is the one that hits Suhail's two books hardest. WTI closed Friday at $95.42 after a week that flirted with $106 — the rupee touched 95.33, Sensex shed 516 points on Friday, and USD/CAD sits at 1.3681 (the "Carney sovereignty trade" hasn't translated into FX yet — the loonie is going the wrong way). India is the Hormuz casualty: D2C brands are being told to switch to cash-and-carry, working capital cycles are collapsing, and pump-price relief from Delhi is conspicuously absent. Canada has the opposite problem: the macro case is improving (Bruce C, oil exports, fiscal flex), but the FX market refuses to vote yet. Both stories are the same Fed dynamic with two faces.

What I'd do if I were repositioning today: under-weight USD/INR exposure (the 96 handle is a coin-flip if Hormuz reignites once more), keep one eye on US S&P enterprise-software earnings for the second "agentic AI" 10%+ cut — that's the one that turns the Cloudflare frame from outlier into wave — and treat Bruce C not as a power story but as Canada's first explicit bid to be the energy-arbitrage backstop for North American AI compute. The next quarter is not about whether AI works. It's about whether the market gives credit for replacing labor with tokens. The answer to that question is currently being voted on, in real time, by Cloudflare's tape.

The Long Game

slow burn — quarters not days
The four pieces of news this week that no one connected — Anthropic's interest in multi-gigawatt orbital AI compute, Pixxel's plan for an India-built orbital data centre satellite, the "Three Mile Island AI revival" narrative, and Bruce C's 4.8 GW commitment — are all bets on the same proposition: the bottleneck for AI in 2027–2030 is not GPUs, it is electricity. Electricity is land-and-grid-constrained on Earth and effectively unconstrained in low-Earth-orbit (modulo the very real cooling problem). Whoever cracks the heat-rejection physics for orbital compute first owns the next decade. Whoever controls 4+ GW of clean baseload near tier-1 fiber owns the decade after. India and Canada are sitting at opposite ends of this curve — India with the spacetech cluster and almost no terrestrial nuclear acceleration; Canada with the inverse. The merger of those two stories is not yet a Brief headline, but it is the most underwritten cross-border AI thesis on the table.
