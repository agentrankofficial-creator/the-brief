THE BRIEF

Uber blew its AI budget in four months

Edition #185 - Sunday, May 24, 2026 - Morning
Global long bonds broke last week in four countries simultaneously, Uber burned its full-year AI budget on Claude Code by April, Microsoft cancels its Claude Code licences by June 30, and a San Francisco startup just sent a humanoid to clean an apartment for $150 a booking.
EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth - May 24, 2026

Two stories landed on Saturday morning that look unrelated and are not. Uber's CTO told The Information that the company burned its entire 2026 AI budget by April - heavy users running Claude Code at $500-$2,000 a month, 84% of engineers classified as agentic users, 70% of committed code AI-authored. Then The Verge reported Microsoft is cancelling internal Claude Code licences by June 30 and pushing everyone back to GitHub Copilot CLI. The story isn't that AI is too expensive. The story is that an inference-priced tool, when it works, migrates from S&M-style discretionary spend to COGS-style structural cost faster than enterprise procurement can re-model it. Uber's CFO didn't budget for Claude winning. Microsoft can't politically be the company whose engineers refuse its own tool.

Now widen the lens. Bond markets broke last week, simultaneously, in four countries. Barclays' Ajay Rajadhyaksha to John Authers: "Same trade - get me out of duration." The G7 is borrowing at rates the EM enjoyed in the early 2020s. India's rupee printed a fresh closing low at 96.83 on Wednesday before recovering to 96.20; RBI announced a $5B swap auction for Tuesday May 26. Canada's 10Y GoC at 3.74% is a 2-year high, and BoC has to make a call on June 10 with CAD weak. These are the SAME story - a regime where structurally-deflating compute economics meet structurally-inflating sovereign duration economics, all happening as the AI cost-line migrates onto every CFO's income statement.

What I think: the next 60 days produce visible disclosure-regime change. At least one US large-cap breaks out "AI compute" as a separate line on Q2 earnings - Uber and Microsoft have made it impossible for analysts not to ask. And in India, the dollar drain on AI inference (Inc42 ran it Saturday - 7 GW of AI compute needed by 2030, all priced in USD) becomes the single most-cited rationale when the Sitharaman cyber panel publishes its Mythos recommendations. Cross-border operator note: the same dollar-Fed-bond-breakout dynamic is squeezing INR and CAD in parallel. If you're hedged in one but not the other, you're hedged in neither.

SIGNAL SPOTLIGHT
Sarama just shipped the first consumer-scale AI model trained on a substrate that nobody else has tried - data that does not exist in writing - and the method is what travels, not the dog collar.
Alex Wissner-Gross writing in Innermost Loop on May 23: Sarama (founded by Praful Mathur, three-time consumer-supply-chain founder) shipped a dog collar that trains a separate model on each individual dog's life, with the sensors self-labelling each other - camera frames the food bowl, motion logs the lunge, microphone catches the bark, three readings labelling one. Reported 93% accuracy on emotion and intent today, targeting 1M annotated bark sequences by year-end. Earth Species Project and Google DolphinGemma trained on researcher-collected archives; Sarama is the opposite - an instrument in the home generating the dataset. Every prior AI substrate inherited a record humans had already written down. This one has to be measured into existence. The dog is just the first voiceless mind; the same method reads a horse's lameness, a herd's distress, a wildlife population, and eventually any organism we have not learned to listen to. The structural insight is the method, not the SKU.
Time horizon: 18-36 months for the method to compound across species; 6-12 months for the first follow-on raise that prices Sarama as a foundation-model company rather than a pet-tech accessory.

THE LEAD
AI - ENTERPRISE COST up 6-12 MONTHS ACT
Uber blew its entire 2026 AI budget in four months on Claude Code - and that's the moment the inference economy stopped being a finance problem and started being a P&L category

Uber CTO Praveen Neppalli Naga told The Information that the company exhausted its full-year 2026 AI budget by April, with heavy users running between $500 and $2,000 per engineer per month on Claude Code. By March, 84% of Uber engineers were classified as agentic coding users; ~95% used AI tools monthly; ~70% of committed code came from AI systems. Total 2026 R&D spend: $3.4 billion. Naga is now "back to the drawing board" and is openly modelling a shift to "agent engineers" - AI systems that handle coding, testing and deployment with other AI tools supervising the process.

SO WHAT: The inference-priced AI tool is the first major enterprise software cost line that scales with usage rather than per-seat - which means it migrates from S&M-style discretionary to COGS-style structural the moment adoption crosses ~60% of the engineering org. CFOs across both India and Canada portfolio companies need to re-model AI as compute-rent, not software-license, and the disclosure cycle will force this in Q2 earnings. For Indian IT services with US-customer exposure (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL), the question flips: does AI compress your billable-hour count or get repriced as a passthrough? Canadian portfolio companies running on AWS Bedrock or GCP face the same FX leakage Inc42 flagged for India.
ACTION: Watch for the first US large-cap to break out "AI compute" as a separate line item in Q2 earnings (likely late July). When that disclosure hits, the analyst question becomes universal and the next earnings cycle delivers it across the board. For private Indian / Canadian operators using inference-priced AI tools, lock 12-month commitments at current pricing before vendor structures get changed.
The counterargument: Uber's number isn't AI being expensive - it's Uber's procurement being naive. Heavy users at $2,000/month produce code worth $20,000/month at salary cost; the unit economics are 10x positive. The real story is that ENTERPRISE COST CONTROLS failed, not that AI costs broke. If Microsoft was killing Claude Code on cost, GitHub Copilot CLI wouldn't be a Microsoft product either - this is anti-trust positioning dressed as cost discipline.

The number that matters is the ratio: 95% monthly usage x 70% AI-authored code commits = a tool that has crossed the "ambient utility" threshold inside Uber. At that point you cannot ration it without destroying productivity, and you cannot budget it linearly without misforecasting by an order of magnitude. The Ken's Praveen Gopal Krishnan reads this correctly: this is the end of free compute and the beginning of better engineering - companies will now make smarter routing decisions about where to deploy expensive tokens. Microsoft chose Copilot. The interesting question is which other Big Tech does the same in the next 60 days.

FRONTIER AI - SUBSTRATE up 12-24 MONTHS NOTE

SO WHAT: This is the first AI substrate where the training data has to be measured into existence rather than scraped from a written record. For operators in agriculture, veterinary services, or wildlife conservation, this is the underlying primitive worth tracking. ACTION: Add Sarama to the watchlist as a foundation-model-company-not-pet-tech-accessory. Track first 50,000-collar shipment milestone or pet-brand distribution partnership.

Bloom-Groshen-Hobbs-Strain put a number on the McEntarfer firing cost: $100B+ GDP, 168,000 fewer non-farm payrolls - the price of data-credibility loss is now measurable

Adam Tooze surfaced a Nicholas Bloom (Stanford) / Erica Groshen (Cornell, former BLS head) / Duncan Hobbs / Michael Strain (AEI) working paper estimating that the August 1, 2025 Trump firing of BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer produced a 50% week-over-week jump in the Economic Policy Uncertainty index, which translated to approximately $100B in lost US GDP and 168,000 fewer non-farm payrolls. The authors estimate BLS produces $25 of economic value per $1 spent on its ~$700M budget. CPS response rate has fallen from ~90% a decade ago to under 70%; Consumer Expenditure Survey response from 68% to 40%.

SO WHAT: The data-credibility cost is no longer abstract. For any operator forecasting on official US economic statistics, the implied confidence interval widened materially in 2025-26. India's NSO and Canada's StatCan have not experienced equivalent shocks, but the precedent is the precedent - government-statistics integrity is now in the political-risk category alongside trade policy.
ACTION: Add credibility-of-statistics as an explicit assumption when forecasting on official prints. For India NSO data and Canada StatCan data, note whether release schedules / revisions have been disrupted.
The counterargument: the EPU-to-GDP elasticity is the contested part of the paper. The headline number is high; the actual causation is one of several explanations for the August-September weakness.

THE LONG GAME
Two adjacent stories make a quiet pattern. The Ken's Praveen Gopal Krishnan calls "the end of free compute" - the moment companies start engineering token-spend rather than provisioning it. Innermost Loop announces Sarama - the first AI substrate where the training data has to be measured into existence rather than scraped. Read together, they sketch the next phase of the AI economy: compute becomes the input that engineers optimise around (not assume), and data becomes the input that operators instrument for (not borrow). The companies that win the next 36 months are the ones that internalise both - they neither overpay for inference nor under-invest in proprietary data collection. The ones that lose are the ones still living in the 2024-25 paradigm of "free tokens against scraped web." Worth tracking: which Indian and Canadian operators are quietly deploying instrumentation that creates proprietary datasets (industrial sensors, embedded camera networks, behavioural logging). Those are the foundation-model companies of 2028 in disguise.
