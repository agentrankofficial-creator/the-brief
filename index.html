Editor's Read
Suhail Seth · May 9, 2026 (Evening)

Today's news is two jobs reports printed within an hour of each other on opposite sides of the 49th parallel, and the gap between them is the story of 2026 in microcosm. The US added 115,000 jobs in April, double the consensus, while Canada lost 18,000 and tipped into a 6.9% jobless rate — a six-month high. Same continent, same Hormuz oil shock, same dollar regime, completely different labor outcomes. The decoupling everyone is pricing into CAD/USD isn't sovereignty — it's distress.

This matters because the consensus narrative on Canada has been the Carney "Sovereign Canada" trade — pipelines, parallel optionality, refusing to pay a CUSMA entry fee. That story works in op-eds. It does not work in the labor data. Full-time employment is now -111K YTD; Quebec just shed 43,000 jobs in a single month; youth unemployment is 14.3%. The June 10 BoC decision (P162-2 in our tracker) just got a much friendlier setup for a cut to 2.0%. USD/CAD at 1.3681 is moving in the opposite direction of our P159-2 / P152-2 1.35 target — and the prediction is now structurally wrong unless the US labor print breaks.

Stitch it across to India and the rhyme is sharper. INR closed 94.58 today (31 paise weaker on the Hormuz fire-exchange, well within our P163-1 95.50 watch zone), 10Y G-Sec back to 6.96%. India and Canada are running the same playbook from opposite ends — both bleeding on a strong-USD/oil-spike combination, both with central banks that have now lost the ability to cut without crucifying their currency. Two petro-adjacent EM/DM hybrids being whipsawed by a US that is simultaneously firing on Iranian tankers AND brokering a 9.9% government stake in Intel that just printed a $50B+ paper gain in a single session. That is what state capitalism looks like when it works. The forward call: if the May 8 tanker fire converts into a third Hormuz Yo-Yo cycle within seven sessions, Brent prints $108 and the BoC cuts 50bp on June 10 instead of 25 — the bigger move is now the high-probability move.

AI · MACRO↑2026–2031WATCH
Goldman puts the AI build at $7.6T through 2031 — and the four assumptions that move the number by hundreds of billions

Goldman's new tracker pegs cumulative AI-infrastructure spend at $7.6T over 2026–2031 — $765B in 2026 alone, scaling to $1.6T/year by 2031. The bigger insight isn't the number; it's the four supply-side variables that swing it: (1) chip lifespan — accountants depreciate over 4–6 years but NVIDIA ships new architectures annually, so real economic obsolescence may be 3 years; (2) data-center cost — old cloud DCs ran ~$10M/MW, new AI ones cost $15–20M/MW, and even 2-year-old facilities can't house next-gen chips; (3) chip mix (GPUs vs ASICs) — NVDA's 75% GPU margins vs cheaper ASICs net out because cheaper compute drives more compute usage; (4) bottlenecks — transformers, interconnects, specialized labor stretch timelines without changing unit cost.

SO WHAT (India): India's MeitY ₹76,000 Cr semi mission is sized for ~2% of the global tab. Even at 5% capture, India needs another ₹3 lakh Cr commitment to be relevant. The Skyroot $1.1B unicorn and Pixxel orbital-DC bet are pieces of the right puzzle, but the puzzle is bigger than what's been allocated.

SO WHAT (Canada): Bruce C nuclear (4,800 MW) is the energy moat. The compute moat is empty. Carney's June "Sovereign Compute" announcement (P163-4) needs ≥CAD$1B to even register against this scale.
ACTION: If you're allocating to picks-and-shovels: power, glass, and rare cooling stays the trade. NVDA-Corning ($500M warrants for 15M shares + 10x optical capacity, 3 new factories) is the template — find the next analog in immersion cooling or HVDC.
💭 The contrarian read: Goldman is supply-modeling a number that the AI demand side cannot honor. The Long Game
The structural story of 2026 is shaping up to be the bifurcation of state capitalism. The US has discovered (via the Intel 9.9% stake and Apple/Intel deal) that direct equity participation in strategic industries can produce tens of billions in paper gains while accomplishing industrial-policy ends. India is mid-stride in copying the playbook (PLI as cash subsidy, but the next iteration will be PLI-with-equity). Canada is talking about it (Carney sovereignty narrative) without doing it. The EU is structurally banned from doing it (state-aid rules). China has been doing it for 20 years. The countries that adopt sovereign-equity industrial policy at scale in 2026–27 win the next decade of frontier-technology cap-table gravity. The countries that don't are subsidizing customers who will. Worth watching as a single multi-quarter trade.
