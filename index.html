EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 25, 2026 · Evening

Today is the most consequential single day for Canadian sovereignty in 2026 so far, and almost nobody is connecting the three threads that ran through Ottawa simultaneously. Cohere released Command A+ on May 20 — 218 billion parameters, MoE, runs on two H100s, the first fully Apache 2.0 frontier model from a Canadian lab. Aidan Gomez's framing was explicit: "open source for sovereign critical infrastructure." Bill C-22 — the lawful-access surveillance bill that would force backdoor access and year-long metadata retention — went into committee with Signal, NordVPN, Windscribe and at least implicitly Apple and Meta threatening to leave the country if it passes. And at 9:00 this morning Piyush Goyal walked into Ottawa with 100-plus business chiefs (mining, energy, automotive, aerospace) — the largest delegation India has ever sent to any country — to push CEPA toward a CAD$70B bilateral trade target by 2030.

One government cannot simultaneously be the Apache-2.0 sovereign-AI champion, the surveillance state that drives away the privacy stack, and the trade partner courting the world's largest tech-services market. Carney's team has not yet been forced to choose, and it is going to be forced this quarter. My read: Bill C-22 gets quietly amended out of the backdoor and metadata clauses by August — the same Liberal pattern as the Online News Act climbdown — because the contradiction with the Cohere-Goyal-CEPA story is too visible. The structural angle that nobody is pricing yet: Cohere Apache-2.0 is a giveaway to second-tier sovereign-AI labs in India, Korea and the UAE, and the procurement implication is that Anthropic's four-vendor silicon hedge from last week is the wrong shape entirely. The right hedge is open-weight model + your own foundry — which is exactly the CPFC photonics privatisation thesis that has been building since the Joly announcement in early May.

On the macro side: WTI dropped 5% to roughly $91 on Trump-Iran "closer to a deal" messaging, the rupee firmed to 95.96 on the RBI ref rate, the TSX printed a fresh all-time intraday high of 34,574.87 on Friday, and SpaceX put a $28.5 trillion TAM in its S-1 — a number so absurd Stanford's Ryan Cummings called it "farcical" and the AI portion alone ($26.5T) exceeds 2024 US GDP. The IPO prices June 12. If you are an operator running cross-border exposure, three questions: do you trim CAD/USD here at 0.7236, do you front-run the BoC June 10 cut, and do you want any SpaceX retail allocation when the prospectus reads like a TAM-fiction novella. My answers: yes, yes, and only if the allocation is reasonably priced — the underlying business is real, the TAM number is theatre.

THE LEAD

CANADA · SOVEREIGNTY↑this weekACT
Three threads converged in Ottawa today — Cohere Apache 2.0, Bill C-22 surveillance, Goyal CEPA delegation — and Carney's government cannot run all three at the same time

Within a five-day window Canada produced a perfect contradiction. Cohere released Command A+ on May 20 — a 218B-parameter MoE frontier model with 25B active parameters, native citations, W4A4 lossless quantization, runs on two H100s — under full Apache 2.0 license, the first fully open-weight frontier model from any Canadian lab. Aidan Gomez framed it explicitly as "open source for sovereign critical infrastructure." Then this morning, Piyush Goyal walked into Ottawa with more than 100 senior business reps from mining, energy, automotive and aerospace — what he called "the largest-ever business delegation to go from India to any country" — to push the India-Canada CEPA toward a CAD$70 billion bilateral trade target by 2030. Goyal meets Carney and trade minister Maninder Sidhu in Ottawa Mon, then runs a two-day Toronto programme Tue–Wed. And sitting under all of it: Bill C-22, the lawful-access surveillance bill that would force backdoor data access and year-long metadata retention. Signal has publicly committed to leaving Canada, Windscribe is relocating its HQ, NordVPN warned it would follow, and Apple and Meta have raised formal objections. US House Judiciary and Foreign Affairs chairs sent a letter to Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree warning the bill threatens US national security and cross-border data flows.

SO WHAT · A government cannot credibly run a sovereign-AI champion, an authoritarian surveillance bill, and a trade-thaw with India simultaneously. The Cohere release implicitly promises India and other partners that Canadian sovereign-AI is open and transparent. Bill C-22 says the opposite. Goyal's delegation is here partly to test that Canada is a serious tech-trade counterparty. Carney's choice is no longer hypothetical — he must amend or kill C-22's backdoor and metadata clauses by September or the Cohere-CEPA narrative cannot survive its own promise.
ACTION · Watch the C-22 committee schedule next 4 weeks. Look for (a) backdoor + retention clauses quietly amended out — bullish for Canadian tech, neutral for Cohere; (b) US Congress escalation that turns C-22 into a CUSMA grievance — bearish for CAD; (c) Goyal-Carney joint statement Tue specifically mentioning data/privacy frameworks — read as a CEPA gating item.
💭 The Liberal government has demonstrated repeatedly (Online Streaming Act, Online News Act) that it will stand behind unpopular tech legislation past the point of reasonable trade or industry cost. The same playbook could simply hold here: C-22 passes substantially as drafted, the tech-exodus rhetoric is mostly posture, Cohere is too small to define national policy, and CEPA gets signed anyway because India needs Canadian critical minerals more than it cares about Canadian privacy law.
