Editor's Read
Suhail Seth · June 6, 2026

Friday gave us the cleanest contradiction of the year, and almost no one is naming it. The Nasdaq fell 4.18% — its worst single day since April 2025 — gold dropped to its 2026 low, and bitcoin slid under $64k. The trigger wasn't an AI disappointment. It was the opposite of a problem: 172,000 new American jobs against an expected 88,000. A strong labour market revived the one thing the AI bull case can't price — a Fed that hikes instead of cuts. Yields jumped toward 4.5%, the dollar firmed, and every long-duration bet — chips, gold, crypto — got repriced in the same breath.

Here's what I think the tape is missing. On the very same day the market was dumping AI equities, Google agreed to pay SpaceX $920 million a month — through June 2029 — for raw compute. Meta is reportedly lining up tens of billions in fresh stock to fund data centres. Trump said out loud he wants the US government to own a piece of the AI labs. Read those together: the capex commitments to AI are accelerating while the equity multiples on AI are cratering. That gap is the whole story. The selloff is a repricing of who keeps the profit, not a vote that the spending stops.

For an operator sitting in both Delhi and Toronto, the second-order move matters more than the index. A hawkish-again Fed is dollar-positive, and that lands squarely on the two currencies I live in: the rupee is parked near 95 and the loonie near 72 cents, both hit by the same Washington dynamic, not by anything local. Macklem walks into June 10 with a weaker currency and a hotter US print pulling against any cut. My read: the durable winners of this AI cycle are the toll-collectors with signed contracts, not the labs with paper valuations. Watch what gets built, not what gets quoted. Does next week's bounce — if it comes — lead with the landlords or the labs?

The toll-booth gets its cheque whether or not the index is green. The market is repricing the distribution of AI profit, not the existence of AI spend.

The Lead
MARKETS▼Days–weeksACT
The AI trade just had its worst day since April 2025 — and it was a good jobs report that pulled the trigger

The Nasdaq sank 4.18% to 25,709.43 on Friday, its steepest one-day drop in over a year; the S&P 500 lost 2.64% to 7,383.74 and the Dow shed 695 points to 50,866.78. The cause was not an AI stumble — it was strength elsewhere. US employers added 172,000 jobs in May against forecasts near 88,000, unemployment held at 4.3%, and a labour market that refuses to crack revived bets that the Fed's next move is a hike, not a cut. Treasury yields jumped toward 4.5%, the dollar firmed, and semiconductors — the most rate-sensitive corner of the tape after Broadcom failed to lift its AI-chip outlook — led the rout. Gold fell to a 2026 low and bitcoin slid under $64k in the same risk-off wave.

SO WHAT: This is a discount-rate shock, not a demand shock. When the risk-free rate reprices higher, the most long-duration assets — frontier-AI equities, zero-yield gold, crypto — get marked down first and hardest, regardless of fundamentals. For an India/Canada operator the read-through is currency: a hawkish-again Fed firms the dollar, which is precisely why the rupee sits near 95 and the loonie near 72 cents. Both weakened on Washington, not on anything domestic — the same single mechanism hitting two of the world's petro-/EM-linked currencies at once.
ACTION: Separate the AI capex names (compute, power, infrastructure — paid contractually) from the AI multiple names (model labs, momentum semis — priced on sentiment). The first is a rate story you can ride through; the second is where the drawdown lives. Watch June 10 BoC and the next US CPI as the swing factors.
💭 Here's the version where this is wrong: one hot payroll print is noise, not trend. If next CPI comes in soft, the "Fed hikes" narrative evaporates as fast as it appeared, Friday looks like a one-day VIX spike, and the dip-buyers who stepped in front of a −4% chip day get paid handsomely by month-end.
Read original → Yahoo Finance →
The semiconductor slide alone wiped roughly $1 trillion in market value across the week. The mechanism is textbook: AI equity valuations embed years of future cash flows, so they carry the longest "duration" of any sector. A 30–40bp move higher in the 10-year discounts those distant flows more steeply than a utility's near-term dividend. The tell that this is rates and not AI: the selloff hit gold and bitcoin — assets with nothing to do with chip demand — just as hard. The bull case survives only if inflation cools enough to take "hike" off the table; the May jobs strength makes the June CPI the most important print of the summer.

AI INFRASTRUCTURE▲3-year contractWATCH
Google will pay SpaceX $920M a month for compute — the clearest sign yet that AI spend keeps climbing while AI stocks fall

A regulatory filing released Friday — one week before SpaceX's planned Nasdaq IPO — shows Google agreeing to pay SpaceX $920 million a month for roughly 110,000 Nvidia GPUs plus CPUs and memory housed in SpaceX data centres, running October 2026 through June 2029. Google pays the full rate from October, with capacity ramping at a reduced fee through September. If SpaceX misses the committed GPU delivery by September 30, Google can walk after a one-month grace period. Google framed it as a short-term bridge to meet higher-than-expected demand for its Gemini Enterprise platform; it mirrors a deal SpaceX struck last month with Anthropic.

SO WHAT: This is the toll-booth thesis made literal. A hyperscaler with its own TPUs is still renting third-party GPUs at nearly $1B/month — proof the compute shortage is real and that capacity owners get paid on contract, not on sentiment. It also recasts SpaceX: days before listing, it's demonstrating a recurring, multi-year compute-revenue line, not just a launch business. The durable AI margin pools sit with whoever owns power and silicon — a dynamic equally relevant to anyone weighing data-centre exposure in India or Canada.
💭 The bear read: a $920M/month "short-term bridge" with exit clauses after Dec 31 is exactly the kind of capex that gets cut first if Gemini demand disappoints — and a deal dropped the week before an IPO is at least partly a valuation prop for SpaceX's float.
Read original → CNBC →
The structure is a classic capacity hedge: Google can terminate immediately (after a one-month cure) if delivery slips past Sept 30, or accept a reduced amount at prorated fees; after Dec 31, 2026 either party can exit on 90 days' notice. That optionality is the giveaway that this is a stopgap for a demand spike Google can't yet self-supply. The fact that the GPUs sit in SpaceX/xAI-linked facilities knits Musk's ventures into Alphabet's AI supply chain just as the two compete in models — a reminder that at the frontier, compute scarcity overrides rivalry.

AI / STATE CAPITALISM→QuartersWATCH
Trump says the quiet part out loud — Washington wants to own a slice of the AI labs, and OpenAI is already at the table

President Trump confirmed he is considering having the US government take equity stakes in leading AI developers, telling reporters he'll discuss a "partnership" with executives as soon as next week: "pieces could be given to the American public, where the American public essentially becomes a partner with the companies." NOTUS first reported senior officials held preliminary talks; OpenAI's Sam Altman has floated donating equity to seed a "Public Wealth Fund," an idea in OpenAI's April policy proposal. The administration has already taken $2 billion in stakes across nine quantum-computing firms, with IBM's share near $1 billion. Anthropic, notably, says it is not discussing equity transfers.

SO WHAT: The US is extending a now-familiar industrial-policy template — federal equity, then customer-steering — from semiconductors and quantum into frontier AI. When the state becomes a shareholder, the dominant variable for a position stops being "best fundamentals" and becomes "which company does Washington want to win." That reshapes how a global investor screens US AI exposure, and it's the same playbook worth watching for who the government anchors next.
💭 The skeptic's case: "look into it" is not a term sheet. Federal equity in a private $900B-marked lab raises antitrust, governance and constitutional questions that could stall any deal for years — this may be a talking point, not a transaction.
Read original → CNBC →
The precedent chain is now three deep: a Treasury stake in Intel (2025), the $2B quantum-firm package, and now AI labs. Each step normalises the next. The structural question is whether the US is building a sovereign-wealth-style claim on its own strategic tech — closer to the state-capitalism model it criticises in Beijing. For the labs, accepting federal equity could buy regulatory cover and procurement; refusing it (Anthropic's stance) preserves independence but forgoes the moat of being a national champion.

AI SAFETY↑~2 yearsNOTE
Anthropic asks the industry to keep the brakes within reach — Claude now writes 80% of its own code, and the labs say recursion is near

In a June 4 post, "When AI Builds Itself," Anthropic called for the world to preserve the option to "slow or temporarily pause frontier AI development," warning that models are nearing "recursive self-improvement" — the point where AI designs and builds its successors with little human input. Claude now writes more than 80% of the code merged into Anthropic's systems (up from low single digits before Claude Code shipped in early 2025), and engineers ship ~8x more code per quarter than a few years ago. Co-founder Jack Clark said some models could be capable of recursive self-improvement within two years. Anthropic would pause only if rival frontier labs did so verifiably.

SO WHAT: The conditionality is the real message: Anthropic won't unilaterally slow because that just hands the lead to a competitor — a textbook collective-action trap. That's why a binding multi-lab pause is unlikely without a government to enforce it, which loops straight back to Washington's sudden interest in owning the labs. The "80% of code" figure is also the cleanest public datapoint yet on how fast AI is compounding inside the firms that build it.
💭 The cynical read: a safety warning that doubles as a capability flex ("our AI is so good it builds itself") is excellent marketing the week your compute deals and valuation are in the headlines. Calls to pause that bind no one cost nothing.

AFRICA· POLITICS↓NOTE
South Africa's jobs crisis turns violent — Ramaphosa dispatches envoys as neighbours evacuate citizens from xenophobic attacks

President Ramaphosa is sending special envoys across the continent to contain fallout from weeks of attacks on migrants in Africa's biggest economy, where nearly one in three people is jobless. Mozambique said five of its citizens were killed; Kenya, Lesotho and Zimbabwe issued advisories; Ghana, Malawi, Mozambique and Nigeria arranged emergency evacuations. Elsewhere, Kenya's Ruto backed a contested US-built Ebola quarantine facility despite a court order, and Ghana's president detailed the budget hole left by USAID's shutdown (~$154M/year).

SO WHAT: South Africa is a live case of how an unemployment crisis metastasises into a regional-diplomacy and supply-chain risk — Africa's most industrialised economy exporting instability to its neighbours. The USAID withdrawal compounds it: as Western aid retreats and pivots to defence, middle-power African states are being forced to self-finance health and security shocks, a structural shift for anyone with African exposure.
💭 Counterpoint: episodic xenophobic violence has flared in South Africa before without altering the investment case; envoys and advisories may calm it within weeks, leaving the macro picture unchanged.

The Long Game
The slow-burn trend under everything this week is the financialisation and nationalisation of compute. In five days we saw compute rented at $920M/month on a multi-year contract, a hyperscaler reaching for tens of billions in equity to build more of it, a government openly courting ownership of the firms that run it, and a leading lab warning that the systems are starting to build themselves. Compute is becoming simultaneously an asset class (contracted, tradeable cash flows), a strategic state interest (federal equity, export controls, alliance demands), and an autonomous force (recursive self-improvement). When the same resource is being securitised, sovereign-claimed, and self-replicating all at once, the second-order effects — on energy markets, on national-champion policy, on who can even afford to compete — will define the next decade more than any single model release. Watch the contracts and the equity stakes, not the demos.
