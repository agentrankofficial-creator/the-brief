EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 7, 2026

The day everyone called the bottom on Iran, the market called the top on caution. S&P 7,365 and Nasdaq 25,839 both closed at fresh records on Wednesday — the only data point that matters this morning. The proximate cause is Trump pausing Project Freedom and Iran "reviewing a one-page MoU." The actual cause is six weeks of compounding fear releasing in two sessions.

Here is the connection nobody is making. The same risk-off flow that pushed Indian and Canadian assets down in April is now reversing — but asymmetrically. The CAD has appreciated to 1.3583 against USD, a 90-day high. The INR is still pinned at 95. Both currencies were sold on Hormuz; only one is being bought back on the de-escalation. That tells me the rupee weakness is no longer purely an oil-premium story — it has migrated into a structural-flow story (FPI exit, FDI gate widening to absorb Chinese minority capital, RBI passivity). The Carney trade and the Modi trade have decoupled.

Second connection: Apple opening iOS 27 to Google and Anthropic for Apple Intelligence, the same week OpenAI/Microsoft/AMD/Broadcom/Nvidia/Intel publish an open compute-networking standard, the same week Anthropic ships ten finance-vertical agents. The model layer is commoditising in front of us. The moat is moving down — to compute fabric and proprietary data — and up — to distribution. If you are building a wrapper, you are dead by Q4.

The forward call: the market is pricing a peace that has not been signed. I think the snapback risk is a Hormuz incident inside the next 14 days that puts WTI back above $105 and the rupee through 96. The trade is to fade this rip on commodity-sensitive Indian financials, not chase it.

Time horizon: 6–18 months — watch the iOS 27 GA disclosure docs in September.

Apple unbundles itself: iOS 27 will let users route Apple Intelligence to Google or Anthropic — Cupertino has effectively conceded the model layer

Bloomberg reports Apple will ship "Extensions" in iOS 27, iPadOS 27, and macOS 27 this fall. Users will pick a third-party AI provider in Settings — initially Google and Anthropic — to power Siri, Writing Tools, and Image Playground. Apple will continue to offer in-house models alongside, with a disclaimer absolving Cupertino of responsibility for third-party output. SO WHAT: The AI moat just moved. The model layer is now a commodity routed through a settings toggle. The two remaining moats are (1) compute fabric and proprietary data, and (2) the surface that owns user attention. Apple is a surface, not a model. Anthropic and Google just got handed the keys to 1.4 billion devices. India angle: Indian SaaS and consumer apps that built India-specific Apple Intelligence wrappers now compete against Anthropic and Gemini directly inside the OS. Canada angle: Cohere is conspicuously absent — every quarter Cohere is not on the iOS Extensions list is a quarter the Canadian sovereign-AI thesis weakens.
ACTION: If you have an AI-wrapper SKU in either book, kill it before iOS 27 ships in September. Reallocate to data-as-moat plays (proprietary verticals, integration-heavy enterprise). Add Cohere to the Canada watchlist — its inclusion or exclusion in Extensions by WWDC June is the binary signal.
💭 Counter-thesis: Apple has done this dance before. "Extensions" may end up like CarPlay — technically open, practically dominated by Apple's defaults. Most users never change a setting. ↗ OpenAI MRC protocol ↗ Truce trade
AI / INFRA ↑ 12 MONTHS NOTE
OpenAI publishes MRC compute protocol with Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Nvidia, Intel — the entire frontier just standardised on one networking stack

OpenAI and a coalition of researchers from Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Nvidia, and Intel published a joint paper on Multipath Reliable Connection (MRC), a networking protocol that uses "packet spraying" to scatter data across hundreds of paths simultaneously, prevents congestion, and reroutes around failures in microseconds. The protocol pairs with SRv6 IPv6 segment routing. It is already running in OpenAI/Microsoft training clusters in Abilene, TX and Microsoft's Fairwater supercomputers, and was used to train GPT-5.5. The spec is open-licensed via the Open Compute Project.

SO WHAT: When the five most concentrated players in AI compute publish a single shared protocol, two things happen — (1) frontier compute becomes cheaper for everyone in the consortium, (2) every smaller lab outside it just got further behind. India's nascent sovereign-compute ambitions and Canada's Cohere/Tenstorrent ecosystem either join the standard or pay a permanent efficiency tax. India action: Pre-budget signals on the National AI Mission compute layer should explicitly endorse MRC compatibility — silence here is itself a signal. Canada action: Watch whether Cohere announces Inference-1 cluster on MRC by year-end.
ACTION: Read the MRC spec on OCP. If you have any data-centre exposure (REIT, infra equity, or hyperscaler vendor), check vendor claims against MRC compatibility — it is the new TCP for AI infra.
💭 Counter-thesis: Open standards in compute have a history of being abandoned the moment one player gets a proprietary edge. Microsoft and OpenAI publishing MRC is partly cover for the fact that their Stargate buildout has hit a power and networking wall they cannot solve alone. The protocol may be open; the chip and power supply chain that runs it absolutely is not.

K · The Long Game
Slow-burn trend The model layer of AI is finishing its commoditisation arc. Apple's iOS 27 Extensions, OpenAI's open-licensed MRC compute protocol, and Anthropic's vertical-agent push all happened inside seven days.
