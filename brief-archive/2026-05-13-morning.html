Beijing lands, Mumbai bleeds, Cerebras prices

Edition #171 · Wed May 13, 2026 · Morning
Trump touches down in Beijing at sundown. The Sensex just lost 1,456 points and ₹95.24 is staring at the wall. Cerebras prices tonight — the biggest US tech IPO of the year. Three signals, one cycle.
EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 13, 2026 · Morning

The single thread running through every screen this morning is the same one Bill Bishop laid out plainly in Sinocism last night: Nvidia's Jensen Huang is not on the CEO delegation flying to Beijing. That single absence tells you more than any joint statement will. Trump wants a win in China. Xi wants stability while he buys time. Neither wants a chip deal big enough to upend the federal-equity-anchor playbook the US has spent the last nine months perfecting. Julian Gewirtz at ChinaTalk called this exactly right when he framed the next three days as a "stalemate summit" — fighting continues, but tensions ease, and both leaders walk away saying they won.

What does this mean if you're sitting in Mumbai or Toronto? In Mumbai it means the Sensex losing 1,456 points yesterday wasn't an overreaction — it was the market pricing in the actual sequence. The rupee at 95.24, foreign investors $21 billion lighter on Indian stocks since February 28 (Chartbook quoting NSDL data), 10-year GoI yields above 7.1% — none of that resolves until Hormuz resolves, and Hormuz resolves through Beijing, not through Modi's austerity asks. Modi telling Indians to skip foreign holidays and pause gold-buying is the policy response of a government that knows it can't fix the price of oil — it can only ration its consequences. That is the inverse of strength. In Toronto, the same arithmetic produces a different ledger: the TSX closed at 34,291 last Monday and reopens today; potash and uranium will catch a bid every time someone mentions Hormuz; CAD/USD around 1.36 punishes nobody in Suhail's PCC business because it's local-cost local-revenue. Canada is the world's least-bad place to be sitting this week, and I don't think the rest of the world has fully priced that yet.

The third leg — Cerebras pricing tonight at the top of its $150-$160 range, raising $4.8B at a $48.8B valuation, the biggest US tech IPO of the year — closes the loop. Compute is being financialized in real time, on the same day the President flies to the country whose chip controls are "largely beginning not to work" (Eric Schmidt's words last week). I think the open tomorrow on CBRS prints between $185 and $210 — strong but not the +30% blow-out that P168-3 needs. The 20x oversubscription gets fully priced into the upper-range pricing tonight; the day-1 distribution begins immediately. My forward call: by Friday's lunch, when Trump's plane lifts off from Beijing, we'll know more about whether ST-005 (the Hormuz-China-India triangulation thesis) gets a regime-flip catalyst or has to wait until July. If the joint communiqué contains any oblique de-escalation language on the Gulf, INR rallies from 95.24 within five sessions. If it doesn't, we're staring at 96.

None of this resolves through fiscal action in Delhi. India imports 90% of its oil and gas; half of crude and two-thirds of natural gas transit through Gulf countries whose exports are shut. The only release valve is Beijing. If Xi delivers anything in the next 72 hours that gives Trump cover to lift the most-punishing Iran sanctions — even just oblique "energy security cooperation" language — the capital flow inverts within two weeks. If he doesn't, July is the next window. This thread is ST-005 in your signal threads dossier; this is its first major firing.
