EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 11, 2026 (Morning)

Three things happened in 36 hours that I think nobody is connecting yet. On Sunday morning, Iran delivered its counter-proposal to the US ceasefire framework through Pakistani mediators. By Sunday afternoon, Trump posted on Truth Social that the response was "TOTALLY UNACCEPTABLE." Brent jumped 3% to ~$104 in early Asia trade. And — separately, in a parallel news cycle — Islamabad spent the day commemorating the one-year anniversary of Marka-e-Haq, the very ceasefire that ended Pakistan's near-war with India on May 10, 2025.

Sit with that for a second. The country that twelve months ago was launching missiles at Indian airbases is now the diplomatic conduit through which Tehran negotiates with Washington over the price of the oil that drives Indian inflation. India's macro-input — every rupee of imported crude, every basis point of CPI, every RBI calibration — is now routed through India's strategic rival. That is a structural asymmetry, not a news cycle.

The Canada read is different but related. WTI closed Friday at ~$95 after a violent week (intraweek high $106 per fxleaders), but the Sunday Trump rejection just reset the trajectory. For Canada, that's a tailwind on the oil-export side and a headwind on the BoC-cut side — Whirlpool-class consumer pain gets harder to "cut through" when crude is back above $100. The TSX printed 34,077.76 on Friday — within sniffing distance of an all-time high — but that was a peace-deal-priced tape, and the Sunday rejection invalidates it. CAD weakened to 1.3706 USD on Friday despite oil strength; if oil goes higher this week, watch whether the loonie can hold 1.36 or whether the BoC-cut pricing reasserts and we test 1.38.

My call: the May 14 deadline on P161-1 (Iran accepts MoU) is now firmly in the WRONG column. P159-1 (no peace by May 21, WTI prints >$105 again) is now my highest-conviction OPEN line. Project Freedom re-activates within 5 sessions (P163-3 leading). For the trade desk: I would not be net-long any Indian importer name into Friday's CPI print, and I would add CAD-puts versus the Canadian energy ETF as a paired structure. The actual signal for the week is on India deeptech — Skyroot's $1.1B unicorn with GIC and BlackRock anchors is the open-the-floodgate trade for Indian deeptech as a global asset class. Watch Pixxel / Agnikul / Bellatrix follow-on rounds in the next 60 days.

INDIA · REGULATORY↑QUARTERSWATCH
SEBI reverses 8-year stance — open-market buybacks return, merchant bankers go optional, promoter shares freeze at depository level

SEBI's consultation paper (public comment open till May 29) reintroduces the stock-exchange route for share buybacks — phased out after the 2018 PC Jeweller fiasco. The new rules: buyback window capped at 66 working days (vs. PMAC's six-month ask), at least 40% deployed in the first half, promoter holdings frozen at ISIN level during open-market buybacks (not just barred from trading), no buyback if it breaches Minimum Public Shareholding, and merchant bankers become optional. The separate buyback trading window is scrapped — buyback-distribution tax was already killed by the Finance Act 2026.

SO WHAT: Three big effects: (1) Cash-rich Indian platform companies (Paytm, Eternal, TCS, Infosys, HCLTech) now have a faster, cheaper buyback path — expect a wave of announcements in H2 FY27. (2) Mid-cap and small-cap PSUs benefit most from merchant-banker optionality. (3) The promoter-freeze rule kills the "announce-and-stuff-friends" signaling game that triggered SEBI's 2018 tightening.
ACTION: Skim portfolio for companies with cash > ₹5,000 Cr and FCF margin > 15% — these are the buyback-window candidates. Treat announcements as more credible now that the 50% completion floor + promoter freeze is structural.
💭 Counter-thesis: 66 days is too short to absorb meaningful capital for India's largest companies. If a TCS or Infosys announces ₹15,000 Cr, daily liquidity caps the executable portion. Open-market buybacks remain a small-cap tool; large-caps will still default to tender offers despite the new optionality.
