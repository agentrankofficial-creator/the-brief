EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 8, 2026 (Evening)

Three things happened in the last 24 hours that I want to put on the same line. First: the US-Iran "ceasefire" became a working fiction. Three US Navy destroyers transited the Strait of Hormuz, Iran fired, the US fired back at targets including Qeshm Island, and Trump told reporters the truce is "still in effect." Markets called the bluff politely — WTI rallied a token +1.95% to $96.66, not the +20% you'd see if anyone believed the war was actually back on. Brent is still $103.

Second: Whirlpool — Whirlpool, the dishwasher company — said Q1 industry shipments fell 7.4%, March alone fell 10%, and the print is "similar to what we observed during the global financial crisis." It suspended its dividend. It cut FY26 EPS guidance from $6.23 to $3.00–$3.50. The CFO said demand hasn't been this low since the GFC and named the Iran war as the cause. That is the actual Iran tax, and it is no longer abstract.

Third: Cloudflare fired 1,100 people — 20% of its workforce — explicitly framed as a transition to an "agentic AI-first operating model." It said internal AI usage was up 600% in three months. The severance is generous (full base pay through year-end, equity vest through August), which tells you management thinks this is a one-time reset, not a slope.

Connect them. The Hormuz risk premium is bleeding into a goods-economy recession (Whirlpool, Shake Shack -28% on beef). The AI productivity boom is, simultaneously, a labour-cost dump (Cloudflare 20%, DeepL 25%). India and Canada split the dividend differently: Skyroot just became India's 129th unicorn at $1.1B with Vikram-1 launching in weeks, and Pixxel announced India's first orbital data centre — sovereign launch + sovereign compute, in one quarter. Canada is paying up for the other half: Ontario signed a $300M cost-share for Bruce C nuclear — potentially the world's largest plant, 4,800 MW. Anthropic just rented SpaceX's 220,000 GPUs and 300 MW of Colossus 1 capacity, and openly asked about "multiple gigawatts of orbital AI compute." If you want the tape's signal: the GPUs are leaving Earth, the goods economy is contracting, and labour is being repriced in real time. The Iran war is the cover, not the cause.

⌛ THE SLOW BURN

Foreign universities are using India as their lifeboat — and it's not working. The Ken's Atul Krishna reports six foreign universities (Deakin, Bristol, Aberdeen among them) just partnered with Eruditus to launch a ₹1,000cr multi-year scholarship. The framing is generosity. The reality is distress: Australian, UK, and Canadian higher-ed are all dependent on the Indian student, and the Indian student is increasingly stuck — visa rejections, family-financial constraints, the broken UK / Canada PGWP-to-PR pipeline. The arrival of foreign campuses inside India (UGC opened the door in 2023) is the panic reaction. But these campuses are operating out of GIFT City corporate buildings; BITS still can't fill seats at its 24-year-old UAE campus; IIT Delhi is going to UAE anyway because rankings matter more than enrolment math. The slow-burn signal: the entire global higher-ed business model — which assumed 30 years of monotonically rising mobility — is rebasing to a world where the visa is the constraint, not the degree. Watch which Canadian universities follow Deakin and Bristol into India by EOY 2026 — and whether the Carney government's response is to subsidise Canadian retention or to subsidise Canadian export. Both are uncomfortable.
