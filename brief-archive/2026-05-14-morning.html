EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 14, 2026 · Morning

This is the day three previously verbal commitments get an actual price tag. Cerebras priced at $185 last night — twenty-five dollars above its already-bumped range, fifty-six billion fully diluted — and the demand book was so deep that the institutional flip dynamic everyone expected got overrun. The Bank of Canada's Summary of Deliberations, published while everyone was watching Trump board Air Force One, used the words "consecutive rate hikes" as a serious scenario for the first time this cycle. And in Beijing today Trump and Xi sit down for the first bilateral — Jensen Huang and Elon Musk literally in the room — and put a number on a US-China relationship that has been pure adjective for eighteen months.

The convergence I want to flag: the CAD rate-cut consensus that has been built into TSX energy positioning and our June-cut framework just took its first crack. The BoC isn't telling Bay Street to expect cuts; it's telling them that if oil-driven inflation spreads through the cost structure, the next move could be a hike — and an explicit plural "consecutive" at that. The TSX closed -210 points at 34,080 yesterday because the rotation that has been priced for a softer-rate path now has to re-underwrite. CAD/USD held 0.730 because the FX market read the shift faster than Bay Street equity desks. The cross-currency book between INR (95.80, fresh record low) and CAD (0.730, holding) just gained meaningful asymmetry — INR weakening on imported inflation that India is now legislating against (gold duty to 15%, +9 percentage points overnight) while CAD is being repriced as a possible hike currency. For a cross-border operator running monthly INR-denominated cost lines AND CAD-denominated revenue lines, the relative-value picture changed yesterday — and almost no one is talking about it because everyone is watching the IPO.

Forward call: I think the Beijing communiqué Friday will be thin on Iran and thick on chips. Jensen's physical presence is the tell. Watch for any line that names rare earths or export licenses — that's where the actual deal will sit. And I'm watching Cerebras's first hour today more than its close: at $185 pricing, the open will tell us whether $56B is the floor or the ceiling of where AI compute-as-asset trades from here.

INDIA · POLICY▲IMMEDIATEACT
India hikes gold and silver import duty from 6% to 15% — Modi's "stop buying gold" appeal became actual customs policy in 48 hours, the fastest verbal-to-fiscal turn this government has executed

The Finance Ministry raised import duty on gold and silver to 15% (10% basic customs + 5% Agriculture Infrastructure Development Cess) effective immediately on Wednesday, May 13 — up from 6%. Platinum went to 15.4%. Domestic gold futures jumped 7.2% to ₹1,64,497 per 10 grams within hours. India is the world's second-largest gold market, and gold is structurally the third-largest line item in the import bill after crude and electronics. The move follows PM Modi's Sunday appeal urging Indians to refrain from gold purchases for a year. The 48-hour gap between rhetorical appeal and fiscal action is unusually fast for North Block.

SO WHAT: A +9 percentage point duty hike is not nudging consumer behaviour — it is taxing the balance of payments back into shape and accepting jewellery-industry pain (the ₹5L crore sector has warned of grey-market substitution). The Indian sovereign just signalled that 95.80 on USD/INR is the political pain threshold for action, not commentary. For any cross-border operator with India-linked import / precious-metal / NRI gold-loan supply-chain exposure, importer-cost models need to re-tile this week. The signal beyond the duty: India is now ten days inside a forex-defence policy window — an NRI bond launch or RBI intervention disclosure within that window has become the base case, not the upside.
ACTION: Re-price India-cross-border deals with gold or precious-metal input exposure. Watch for follow-on NRI bond launch and a possible second duty escalation (silver 18%, platinum 20%) if INR breaks 96. Smuggling-route compliance is now an operational risk for legitimate bullion / jewellery businesses.
💭 Here's the version where this is wrong: the duty hike is a one-week political signal, NRI bond and excise cuts don't follow, INR stays at 95.50-96.00 anyway because the entire move is being driven by Hormuz-priced crude — not gold imports — and the government just bought one news cycle while the underlying BoP math hasn't actually moved. In that world, this is messaging dressed as policy, and FX traders fade the bounce.

CANADA · BOC▲UNTIL JUNE 10ACT
BoC writes "consecutive rate hikes" into the April-29 Summary of Deliberations — first formal acknowledgement of a hiking scenario this cycle, and Bay Street is still pricing the June cut

The Bank of Canada released its April-29 Summary of Deliberations at 13:30 ET on Wednesday May 13. The hold at 2.25% was unanimous. The Council named two divergent risk paths: (1) further cuts if the Iran war and US tariff drag hamper the economy while inflation stays contained, and (2) consecutive rate hikes if oil-shock cost pressures spread through the cost structure. The "consecutive" plural is the key new word — the first time this cycle the Council has formally entertained a hiking sequence in writing. The forecast embedded in the deck still assumes WTI declines to $75 over the year. WTI is $101.50 today. Tightening magnitude in the hiking scenario was described as conditional on the level of energy-sector investment and the path of CAD/USD.

SO WHAT: The June-10 BoC meeting is now a real two-way option. The CAD rate-cut consensus that has been built into TSX rate-sensitive positioning (utilities, REITs, telcos, dividend-stack large-caps) just took its first crack. Operator implication for cross-border businesses with CAD-denominated cost lines: budget for the possibility of no cut in 2026, with a tail risk of a hike. The TSX-Composite -210 yesterday is partly the digestion of this scenario; CAD/USD holding at 0.730 is the FX market reading it faster than equity. The cross-currency arbitrage between CAD (potentially hiking) and INR (definitely weakening) just became a meaningfully real trade direction.
ACTION: Re-stress-test any CAD-revenue / CAD-cost model with a "BoC holds June + hikes by Q3" overlay. CDPQ / OMERS / CPP-IB pension allocators with rate-sensitive fixed-income exposure should be reading this summary in detail.
