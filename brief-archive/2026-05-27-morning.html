Duration is dying

G7 long bonds break to a 19-year peak while the US bombs Iran during the peace talks · Canada-Germany sign LNG, Scotiabank prints +30% · Google's Co-Scientist quietly compresses a year of research into a week.
EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 27, 2026

The bond market just buried the 2% inflation target — and the timing is not subtle. While the 30-year US Treasury yield hits its highest level since 2007 and the entire G7 long-bond complex breaks to multi-decade peaks (Japan an all-time record, UK since 1998), CENTCOM is conducting "self-defence strikes" on missile sites and IRGC mine-laying boats in southern Iran in the middle of Tehran's Qatar talks. Two months into the cycle, the Hormuz Yo-Yo is no longer a cycle — it's the regime. And the curve gets it.

Three threads tighten into one rope. One, the Iran-war energy shock is structural, not transitory: WTI sits at $93 not because the war is ending but because the market has priced a permanent $10-15 risk premium. Trump's Friday "deal is close" framing produced the exact $91 sell-off; Monday's CENTCOM strikes produced the exact +$2 bounce. The dance is now the price. Two, inflation expectations are being repriced through duration, not 5y5y swaps — which Tooze quotes Stuart Kirk on as "noise contaminated by liquidity, QE residue and pension-fund flow." Claudio Borio, former head of the BIS monetary department, calls the next decade "the toughest challenge for central banks in decades." Dario Perkins at TS Lombard: "rate hikes everywhere." Three, the only economic activity rate-insensitive enough to absorb this regime is AI infrastructure capex. Anthropic is paying SpaceX $1.25 billion per month for compute — a $45 billion three-year contract that prices like a sovereign credit, not a tech expense.

For India: USD/INR at 95.77 keeps the RBI's regime-management posture intact, but the West Asia war is now repricing India's D2C supply chain — Inc42 reports packaging, crude-linked inputs and logistics costs hitting margins across the sector. For Canada: TSX slipped 0.5% off Monday's record on Iran "deal" optimism, but the SEFE-Ksi Lisims LNG agreement Carney signs today is the Major Projects Office's first commercial deliverable — Germany buying 1 million tonnes/year of B.C. LNG, routed via Asia not Atlantic. The decoupling thesis just printed its first revenue line.

My call: the 30-year UST closes above 5.25% before July 31 is now my highest-confidence open prediction. The trade is compute-as-rate-immune-asset. Watch for the first S&P 100 to break out "AI compute" on a Q3 earnings call — that's the moment the market starts pricing AI capex like a sovereign, not capex.

⚡ SIGNAL SPOTLIGHT
AI compute is the first industrial asset class that is structurally rate-insensitive — and that's why the 30Y UST can break 19-year highs while AI capex hits record run-rates without breaking the cycle.
Anthropic's $1.25B/month SpaceX contract ($15B annual, $45B over three years) is priced like a sovereign credit, not a corporate opex. The cost of capital that matters for that decision is not the 10-year — it's the marginal token economics versus a labour replacement curve, which is independent of the discount rate. Every other rate-cycle-immune asset class in history (gold, military spend, sovereign debt) needed a state to exist. This is the first one priced by private capital. The implication: a 5.25% long bond does not break the AI capex curve; it accelerates the labour-replacement substitution underneath it. Watch which S&P 100 names disclose AI compute as a line item in Q3 — that's the disclosure regime change.

SO WHAT: Duration is dead as a defensive asset for at least 18 months. Long-duration equity proxies (REITs, utilities, secular growth without margin) lose their bid. Canada is the rare G7 with a fiscal anchor AND an export-side commodity hedge — the CAD outperformance versus duration-heavy currencies (GBP, JPY) is the structural call.
ACTION: If you're carrying long-duration sovereign exposure as ballast, halve it. Replace with short-duration credit + a commodity sleeve (energy + gold). Watch the 30Y UST breach of 5.25% — that confirms the regime. On the equity side: any name where the bull thesis depends on a 4% terminal rate is now a sell.
💭 The bear case: a Hormuz deal genuinely signs by mid-June. Oil unwinds to $75. The duration trade then comes back hard as bond vigilantes get caught short. The Borio/Perkins regime call is built on a single shock (Iran); if the shock unwinds, the curve unwinds with it. But: betting on a clean diplomatic resolution to a Strait of Hormuz war is betting against seven head-fakes in a row.
