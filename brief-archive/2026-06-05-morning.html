EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · June 5, 2026

The headline today is the number — $1.77 trillion for SpaceX, the biggest IPO ever written. But the story I'm actually watching is what the number says about where money wants to sit at this point in the cycle. The largest capital event of the AI era is not an AI lab. It's a launch-and-satellite company. And it prices in the same week that the AI trade sprang two visible leaks: chip stocks rolled over (Broadcom, Micron led a Nasdaq that slipped while the Dow hit a record), and private-credit investors moved to pull cash — 10% from Blackstone's flagship fund, 17% from Cliffwater's. Underneath that, DeepSeek is closing $7.4B at roughly $52B on a cheaper-is-the-product thesis, and even the mathematicians are signing petitions to slow AI down.

Here's the thread connecting them: the Iran oil shock that should have crushed risk has instead been, in Semafor's energy desk's phrase, "remarkably mild" — China's lower imports and US production cushioned it. A war premium that fades frees capital to rotate within the tech complex rather than flee it: out of pay-any-price compute, into hard assets, cheaper AI, and the power that runs it. India and Canada are the same trade from the other side — the RBI held at 5.25% this morning, the BoC almost certainly holds June 10, both petro-linked central banks frozen by one oil-inflation-versus-soft-growth bind, both currencies (₹95.8, C$0.72) carrying the cost.

My call: SpaceX's June 12 debut is the real tell. If a hard-asset megacap pops while chip names chop, the rotation is confirmed — and "AI compute at any price" is officially last season.

⚡ SIGNAL SPOTLIGHT
The undersea cable is the next Hormuz — and Iran just told us so.
Everyone is pricing the oil chokepoint, which is already fading. The leverage Tehran is now floating is different: charging tolls on the data passing through the cables off its shores. Cables in the Strait of Hormuz carry roughly 20% of global data flow. The same week, Australia, the UK and the US agreed new measures — including autonomous submarine drones — to defend the "arteries of modern civilization" against sabotage running at "historically unprecedented" rates. The war's durable legacy won't be the oil premium. It'll be the weaponization of the physical internet, and a new security-spend cycle built around it.
Time horizon: 2–4 quarters

MARKETS · IPO↑ daysWATCH
SpaceX prices the biggest IPO in history — $135 a share, $1.77 trillion, and Musk keeps 82% of the votes

SpaceX set its IPO at $135 a share, valuing the company at $1.77 trillion and raising about $75 billion by selling 555.6 million shares; underwriters can add 83.3 million more for $11.2 billion. That makes it the seventh-largest US company by market cap, above Tesla (~$1.6T), and more than triple Alibaba as the largest US IPO ever. It debuts on the Nasdaq on June 12 under the ticker SPCX, with Musk holding over 82% voting control after the offering. The $1.77T mark assumes the EchoStar spectrum and Cursor transactions close.

SO WHAT: The defining capital event of the AI era is a launch-and-satellite company, not a model lab — a tell about where late-cycle money wants to sit. For India (ISRO / IN-SPACe) and Canada (MDA Space, Telesat), a $1.77T comp resets the valuation ceiling for every sovereign-adjacent space play.
ACTION: Treat the June 12 first-day print as a clean rotation signal — hard-asset megacap versus chip-name chop.
💭 Here is the version where this is the top: a $1.77T valuation on a company carrying AI losses, leaning on two unclosed deals, with 82% founder control and almost no public say — that is a late-cycle liquidity event, not a growth story. If SPCX breaks issue on day one, it is the bell ringing at the peak of the private-market boom.

Oil's "remarkably mild" war — US reserves hit a 2004 low, yet the market learned to shrug it off

US oil reserves fell to their lowest level since 2004 as the Iran war's energy impacts piled up — and yet the shock from the largest supply disruption in history feels "remarkably mild," per Semafor's energy desk. AI · SOCIETY→monthsNOTE
The AI labs and the mathematicians agree on one thing this week — it's moving too fast

THE LONG GAME
Watch the slow handoff from "AI compute" to "AI power" as the scarce input that actually gets priced. For two years the bottleneck story was chips; this week it quietly became electricity — Washington floating a break-up of its largest grid operator, Google paying households to free up megawatts, CATL (a battery maker) underwriting DeepSeek. The next logical step is financialization: when power becomes the gating constraint on the most valuable industry on earth, someone builds the instrument to hedge it. If a major exchange lists a tradeable compute-power contract, that is the moment electricity stops being a utility input and becomes an asset class — and the entire AI capex debate reprices around the cost of the watt, not the chip.
