EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 15, 2026 · Morning

Yesterday produced two signals that contradict each other so directly that one of them has to be ignored, and I want to be on record about which one the market chose to listen to. Same day. Within hours of each other. Trump’s White House cleared H200 exports to ten Chinese firms while Anthropic published a paper titled “2028: Two Scenarios for Global AI Leadership” arguing the only way America wins is to tighten chip export controls, disrupt distillation attacks, and stop “the CCP’s access to compute.” A frontier lab and the White House publicly disagreed on whether the AI cold war is winnable — and the S&P closed above 7,500 for the first time, Nasdaq printed an all-time high at 26,635, Dow retook 50,000, Nvidia ripped +4.4% to extend its monthly rally to 15%. The market priced the trade deal louder than the policy warning. That is the day in a sentence.

The cross-reference I keep coming back to: the same Beijing summit that produced this H200 unlock also produced a new tifa — “constructive China-US relationship of strategic stability” — that Bill Bishop reads as a Chinese framing victory. Beijing now has personal-leader-level cover to recast any future US tightening as a violation of the new framework. Bessent is on CNBC talking about a new “Board of Trade” for the bilateral and a separate “Board of Investment” for non-sensitive areas — new institutions, formally negotiated, while Anthropic’s thesis is that institutions are exactly what shouldn’t exist at this layer. I think Anthropic loses this round on policy and wins it on capital — they’re at $900B trial-balloon, more business customers than OpenAI, and an IPO that the WSJ already has chest-bumping. But on the actual question of whether the H200 stays in America, the answer just got cheaper to question.

Now the cross-border read. India is on the wrong side of this trade today: USD/INR printed a fresh 95.86 record low yesterday, the gold-and-silver duty hike from Tuesday has not stabilised it, the FII outflow regime persists. Canada is on a completely different side of it: Carney and Smith fly to Alberta this afternoon to sign the industrial-carbon-pricing-for-pipeline deal — $130/tonne by 2040 in exchange for an MOU to build a million-barrel-a-day bitumen pipeline to the BC coast, plus a Quebec graphite project from Nouveau Monde Graphite that explicitly targets China’s critical-mineral dominance, plus $800M federal AI spending already deployed including $240M into Cohere. The Canadian play is not export controls. It’s resource and sovereign-stack expansion to fund the AI infrastructure layer directly. If you’re trying to build in AI in 2026, India is the rupee-volatility tax, the US is the policy-incoherence tax, and Canada is the only G7 economy actually building the integrated stack. Watch the Alberta photo-op today — if the announcement names a builder or a route, the Canada-as-AI-infrastructure-supplier theme moves from speculative to consensus.

AI · GEO▲72-HOUR HORIZONACT
Trump clears H200 exports to 10 Chinese firms the same day Anthropic publishes a paper warning that’s how America loses the AI race — Nvidia rallies 4.4%, the policy regime just split in public

On Day 2 of the Beijing summit, the Trump White House cleared Nvidia H200 chip exports to 10 named Chinese firms — the first major chip-export softening since the August 2025 Treasury Intel stake established the federal-equity industrial-policy template. Nvidia closed +4.4%, monthly rally now 15%. The S&P broke 7,500 for the first time, Nasdaq printed 26,635 ATH, Dow retook 50,000 for the first time since February when the Iran war started.

The same day, Anthropic published 2028: Two Scenarios for Global AI Leadership: scenario one is “America has successfully defended its compute advantage” via tighter export controls and disrupting CCP distillation attacks; scenario two is “America has chosen not to act,” AI rules get shaped by authoritarian regimes, “the best models enable automated repression at scale.” Bessent told CNBC the US and China will jointly “set up a protocol in terms of how we go forward with best practices for AI to make sure non-state actors don’t get a hold of these models.” The two visions are not compatible. One side just won the morning.

SO WHAT: The bifurcation is the story. Until yesterday, AI policy in Washington was directionally one-tone — restrict, restrict, restrict. Now the White House is operating a Bessent-Greer trade-deal regime in parallel with a Treasury-Commerce equity-and-control regime, and the two are functionally rivals. For any operator-reader allocating capital to the AI stack: policy variance is now a first-order input on hyperscaler valuations and on which jurisdictions get compute access. India did not get the H200 clearance Chinese firms just got. That asymmetry matters.
ACTION: Track Anthropic’s next public move (Senate testimony, follow-up paper, coalition letter) in the 30 days post-summit. Track whether Commerce or Treasury publicly reviews/rescinds any of the 10 licenses. Either move is the regime tell.
💭 The contrarian read: this isn’t bifurcation, it’s discipline. Trump cleared H200 to commercial Chinese names, NOT to military-end-use ones, while reserving the leverage for future tightening if China doesn’t deliver on agri purchases or critical minerals. Anthropic’s paper isn’t losing the policy fight — it’s positioning for the moment when Beijing breaks the deal and the White House needs an off-the-shelf framework for re-tightening. Both moves are part of the same policy package.
Read CNBC summit takeaways → Sinocism (Bishop) source

The Bishop frame: Bishop’s read on the Day 1 communique is that the new tifa — “constructive China-US relationship of strategic stability” — is a Chinese framing victory. Xi got Trump to personally co-sign language that lets Beijing recast any future US tightening as a violation of a head-of-state agreement. Li Yaqi (cited by Bishop): a relationship you have stabilized is a relationship you can compete inside for a long time without it breaking. This is competition doctrine, not detente.

The Anthropic stake: Anthropic is publicly arguing for a policy regime that protects its own moat. The 34.4% business-customer share (vs OpenAI 32.3% per Ramp) and the $900B WSJ trial-balloon valuation both depend on US frontier compute staying scarce and US-aligned. If H200s reach Chinese firms at scale, distillation accelerates, the moat compresses, the valuation cracks. The paper is policy advocacy AND capital-stack defense.
