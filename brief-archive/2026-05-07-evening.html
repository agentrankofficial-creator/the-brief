Today is the day the Hormuz Yo-Yo broke the wrong way for the bears. Trump paused "Project Freedom" — the US Navy escort op that started Monday — and confirmed a one-page MoU is sitting on Tehran's desk for a 12-to-15-year nuclear-enrichment moratorium in exchange for sanctions relief and full Strait access. WTI cracked from $106 mid-week to $93.21. Brent fell below $103. Equities went vertical: S&P 500 +1.46% to a record, Nasdaq +2.03% to a record, AMD +18.62% on a Q1 beat ($10.25B revenue, datacenter +57% YoY), and Samsung joined the trillion-dollar club, dragging the KOSPI up 6.4%. The Nikkei printed +5%. This is a textbook risk-on melt-up driven by a single binary releasing.

What everyone is missing: the trade today wasn't long-AMD, it was long-rupee and short-CAD-fear. USD/INR fell from 95.16 to 94.18 in a session — the rupee just unwound 11 days of accumulated Hormuz premium in 24 hours. P155-4 (USD/INR >94) called the spike on April 23; today is the round-trip. CAD firmed to 1.3614 from 1.369 a week ago — same dynamic, smaller magnitude, because Canada is the petro-currency that should be HURT by oil normalisation, not helped by it. So why did CAD strengthen? Because the dollar is the real story. Hormuz peace = no more flight-to-quality bid for USD = every EM and commodity currency rips. India is the bigger winner here than Canada.

And then Honda walked. The $15B Alliston EV plant — the largest foreign direct investment in Canadian history when announced in 2024 — has been definitively shelved per Nikkei. This isn't about Hormuz. This is structural: US EV sales fell 27% in Q1 2026 after Trump killed the $7,500 federal credit, fuel-economy rules loosened, and the Carney–Trump trade relationship is too uncertain for Honda to commit. Add GM (BrightDrop killed), Ford (Oakville reverted to gas trucks), and Stellantis (Brampton idled, Ontario battery plant sold) and the Canadian EV strategy is now zero-for-four. Carney's pipeline MOU with Smith hasn't closed either. The signal: Canada is structurally orphaned from the EV transition, and that's a Carney problem he cannot solve with tariff retaliation or a throne speech.

The forward call: I think the Hormuz tail event is now in mean-reversion mode through May 21, and the rupee tailwind is real and durable for 7-10 sessions before any new oil shock. The Canadian auto file, however, is a slow-burn structural negative that won't get priced for weeks. Long INR, neutral CAD, watch for an Ottawa-side announcement of an "Auto-Strategy Reset" — Carney cannot ignore Honda walking. And the iOS 27 Extensions story (see Spotlight) is the underpriced AI structural shift of the day.
