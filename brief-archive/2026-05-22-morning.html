EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · 22 May 2026

Today's gravitational center isn't a market move — it's a ceremony. Kevin Warsh is sworn in at the White House as the 17th Chair of the Federal Reserve, and Jerome Powell does not leave the building. Powell keeps his governor's seat and his FOMC vote — two years still on the term Trump tried to end. The last time a former Fed chair stayed on the board after a hand-over was nearly 80 years ago. Today we get a permanent shadow-chair structure, baked into the institution.

This matters because every other central bank prices off the Fed, and every other central bank now has to model the Warsh-Powell delta — not the Fed's median view, but the variance. You can see it in real time. The RBI is not waiting to find out. On Thursday they intervened in onshore and offshore rupee markets and announced a $5B dollar-rupee swap auction for May 26 — that's a structural reserve-preserving move, not a spot fight. Meanwhile the BoC walks into June 10 with CAD weakening to 1.3776 and 10Y GoC at 3.55%. The Indian and Canadian central banks are both watching the same Washington power balance and pre-positioning differently — RBI defensive on the spot, BoC quietly building hiking-scenario language into its deliberations.

What I think: Warsh's first FOMC will produce more dissent than Powell's last. Miran dissents every meeting. Powell will dissent selectively but loudly. Whoever Trump appoints next will dissent reflexively the other way. The Fed has stopped being a committee with a chair and started being a coalition with a celebrity. The forward call is that this regime change shows up first in the dollar — DXY weakness against gold ($4,541) and BTC ($77K), strength against EM petro-linked currencies (INR, CAD). The trade isn't to bet on a Warsh thesis. The trade is to bet on disorderly variance.

⚡ SIGNAL SPOTLIGHT
The shadow-chair Fed is now a permanent fixture — and EM central-bank defenses like the RBI's May-26 $5B swap auction are no longer event-driven, they're regime-prepositioning
Three weeks ago the RBI was selling spot dollars to defend INR. Yesterday they switched instrument — onshore + offshore spot intervention plus a forward swap auction announced for next Tuesday. Forward swaps preserve reserves while signaling defense; spot sales deplete them. The shift in instrument is the signal: the RBI is no longer fighting a discrete oil shock, it's defending a regime in which the Fed itself is structurally divided and the dollar's path is no longer median-predictable. Expect the BoC to layer similar forward operations into the June 10 statement without naming them as such.

MACRO · FED ↑ multi-year regime ACT
Warsh is sworn in today as the 17th Fed Chair — and Powell stays seated on the FOMC, creating the first formal shadow-chair structure at the Federal Reserve in nearly 80 years

Kevin Warsh, narrowly confirmed by the Senate May 13 on a 54-45 vote — the most divisive Fed-chair confirmation in modern history — is sworn in this morning at the White House by President Trump. The structural anomaly: Jerome Powell, asked to resign by Trump, refused. He retains his governor's seat and his FOMC vote for the remaining two years of his term. The last time a former chair stayed on the board after a hand-over was 1948. Stephen Miran has dissented from every FOMC vote since being seated. At the April meeting, four of twelve members dissented — the most divided the committee has been since 1992.

So What: Every operator with a USD-sensitive balance sheet has to stop pricing the Fed's median view and start pricing the Warsh-Powell delta. In India this shows up as a structurally weaker INR and RBI defense via swap auctions rather than spot intervention. In Canada it shows up as a BoC that retains hiking-scenario language into the June 10 statement even while inflation prints 2.0%. The medium-term import: every Fed meeting from here becomes a binary news event, not a calibration.
Action: Watch the Warsh first-press-conference language for whether he uses Powell-era process vocabulary ("data-dependent", "balanced approach") or breaks with it. The break-vocabulary signal is the cleanest dollar trade. Position for higher cross-asset realised vol; tighten stops on any DXY-pegged carry positions.
💭 The version where this is wrong: Warsh is an institutionalist who shocks markets only with continuity. Powell quietly defers and votes with the median. The "shadow-chair" framing turns out to be Twitter noise and the dual presence professionalises the FOMC. USD volatility compresses, EM currencies relief-rally, and the BoC cuts on June 10 because nothing actually changed.

Historical precedent: Marriner Eccles served on the Fed board for nearly three years after Harry Truman declined to renominate him as chair in 1948 — but Eccles and the new chair (Thomas McCabe) were broadly aligned politically. The Warsh-Powell case is the inverse: appointed by sitting opponents with a publicly hostile relationship. The structural risk is the FOMC drifting from "committee with chair" to "two adjacent camps", each with policy preferences telegraphed publicly. Markets have to repeatedly re-price between Powell's "balanced" framing and Warsh's hawk-flavoured monetarism — and they have no precedent for what a two-power-center FOMC produces in tail events.

Trump's stated rationale for Warsh: faster rate cuts. But Warsh's published record is closer to a hawkish institutionalist (he warned against extending QE in 2010-11, dissented on rate guidance). The market read of Warsh = dovish is mostly wishful thinking based on Trump's stated preference rather than Warsh's body of work.

OpenAI announced on May 20 that an internal general-purpose reasoning model disproved Paul Erdős's 1946 planar unit-distance conjecture in discrete geometry — a problem mathematicians had assumed square grids were optimal for nearly 80 years. The model approached the problem through algebraic number theory and infinite class field towers, not by iterating on grid constructions. Princeton's Will Sawin refined the result; Fields Medalist Tim Gowers called it "a milestone in AI mathematics." Epoch AI's Yafah Edelman moved her median forecast for solving most Millennium Prize Problems forward to 2032. Separately, Innermost Loop reports the White House is briefing labs on an imminent executive order requiring 90-day pre-release notification for frontier models — FDA-style treatment.

So What: The "general-purpose" qualifier matters more than the math. If a non-math-specialised model solves an 80-year discrete-geometry problem via algebraic number theory, frontier models are now generating novel mathematics faster than the human community can verify it. For India and Canada's AI compute-strategy debates, this changes the question — it's not "should we build sovereign compute?" but "do we have a 90-day clock before a US executive order changes export rules?" The answer is structurally bullish for domestic compute pivots.
Action: Watch the executive-order draft language. If it includes export-related provisions (notification of foreign model deployments), it crosses into trade policy. If it's purely domestic safety notification, it's the soft version. The hard version disrupts every cross-border AI deployment plan currently in flight.
💭 The version where this is wrong: the proof is real but narrow. General-purpose models can solve specific algebraic-geometry problems but cannot generalise. The "milestone" framing is over-stated and Epoch AI's 2032 forecast is forecast inflation. The executive order, if it ships, is symbolic and unenforced.

🇮🇳 INDIA · INFRA ↑ years WATCH
India quietly launches a $1.5B Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool with a sovereign guarantee — designed to keep trade flowing when Western insurers withdraw war-risk coverage on 24-hour notice

Per Finshots, India has formally launched the Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool (BMIP) — a $1.5B (₹12,980 crore) insurance arrangement backed by a sovereign guarantee, designed to insure ships and cargo when global insurers withdraw coverage due to sanctions or geopolitical pressure. The structure is two-tier: insurers in the pool plus GIC Re reserves handle claims under $100M; the government guarantee kicks in above that threshold. The coverage extends to foreign vessels carrying Indian-bound cargo, not just Indian-flagged ships. 70% of India's trade by volume and 95% by value moves through maritime corridors.

So What: India is doing exactly the right thing at exactly the right time. War-risk marine coverage can be withdrawn with 24-hour notice; in a Hormuz crisis or Red Sea escalation, that's the single fastest way to disrupt physical trade. The BMIP is also a quiet attempt to recapture the FX-fee leakage that currently flows to London/Singapore/Nordic marine-insurance hubs. The longer-term play is to make Mumbai a marine-insurance hub.
Action: Monitor BMIP utilisation in any actual Hormuz/Red Sea incident — if Indian-bound cargo movement is sustained when Western coverage withdraws, the BMIP works as designed and the next Russian and Iranian oil-trade flows route through Indian-insured ships.
💭 The version where this is wrong: shipping companies don't trust a sovereign-only guarantee in a stress event, the BMIP gets utilised only by Russian flag-of-convenience tonnage, and the $1.5B is treated as a quiet de-facto Russian-trade subsidy rather than a global insurance hub.

⏳ THE LONG GAME
The next 12 months are going to be remembered as the period when AI cost discovery moved from chips to electrons to balance-sheet line items. Three weeks ago Innermost Loop wrote about GPU futures on ICE; this week Nvidia hiked its dividend 25x; today the White House is reportedly drafting a 90-day frontier-model pre-release EO. Each step pushes AI from a "novel-capability" frame into a "regulated industrial input" frame — the same arc that crude oil walked from 1956–1973. The investors who win the next phase are the ones who treat AI compute as an input commodity with a politically-mediated price curve, not a winner-takes-all platform play. For India and Canada the implication is identical: build sovereign compute now, while the political mediation is still being designed, because the rules will set the cost curve for the next two decades.
