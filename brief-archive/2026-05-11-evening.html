Editor's Read
Suhail Seth · May 11, 2026 Evening

Iran called Trump's bluff overnight, and Trump called it back this morning. Tehran's Sunday counter-proposal — keep highly enriched uranium, control the Strait, demand sanctions relief and reparations — was never a serious counter; it was a stalling exercise wrapped in maximalist demands. Trump's "TOTALLY UNACCEPTABLE!" response was therefore predictable. What is interesting is what the tape did next: WTI ripped from the high-$94s into $99.85 (+4.64%), the Sensex shed 1,313 points (−1.70%), the rupee broke to 95.09, and Project Freedom remains paused. The market is pricing the realisation it should have priced two weeks ago — that the Hormuz yo-yo is a regime, not an incident. That's P150-1 vindicated and P159-1 strengthened.

The cross-geography read matters more than the headline. India absorbed the worst of the day because India is the marginal oil importer in this entire equation — 85% imported, INR has no technical floor between 95 and 98, and the Finance Ministry has shown it will not deliver a second excise cut on the Carney template (P153-4 looking shaky). Canada, by contrast, was structurally insulated: oil exporter, CAD around 1.367, TSX held at 34,000+, and on the very same day Carney announced one-year regulatory caps for major projects plus the Canadian Photonic Fabrication Centre spinout that could become — Paul Slaby's words, not mine — "the next TSMC" for optical computing. India is trying to build OpenAI in-house and failing (Krutrim: 550 → 160 staff, chip program dead, LLM paused). Canada is trying to build a foundry and inviting global capital in. One of these strategies has actually worked at scale before.

The forward call: the heterodox toolkit Adam Tooze sketched today — price controls, nationalisation, financial repression, fiscal-monetary coordination — is no longer a left-Keynesian thought experiment. Carney's federal equity in CPFC, Trump's 9.9% in Intel, Anthropic locking up 300MW of SpaceX power and another $1.8B from Akamai, India's stalled sovereign AI experiment — these aren't separate stories. They are one story about which sovereigns are building optionality and which are watching it get built around them. If you operate in both India and Canada, the next 90 days favour the Canadian book and punish the Indian one. Position accordingly.

The Long Game

The Innermost Loop's Wissner-Gross issue this morning carries the throwaway line that maps every other story on this page: "OpenAI and Anthropic are projected to end 2026 with combined ARR exceeding Nvidia's revenue last year." If true, the centre of gravity in the AI economy migrates from silicon to software inside one calendar year — and the entire 2025–2026 hyperscaler-capex thesis (Goldman: $1.15T 2025–2027) is in service of a software layer that has already started to dwarf its enablers. Combine that with Decoupled DiLoCo (Google can now stitch four US data center regions into a single 12B-parameter training run at 2–5 Gbps), Anthropic+SpaceX (orbital data centers via "SpaceXAI"), and the Hermes Agent overtaking OpenClaw on OpenRouter via self-written skills — and the bottleneck is no longer compute, no longer model weights, no longer even data. It is the harness. Whichever sovereign owns the harness owns the next decade. Canada has a small but real shot via CPFC + power + Carney's industrial-policy speed. India had a shot via Krutrim; the shot is gone. The US owns it by default. China is the only contender. Five years from now, "sovereign AI" will look very different from how the policy class is framing it today — and the institutions making the decisions right now will look very dated, very fast.
