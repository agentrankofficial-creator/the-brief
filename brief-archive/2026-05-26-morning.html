EDITOR'S READ
Suhail Seth · May 26, 2026 · Morning

The single most important sentence in this entire news cycle is buried in NVIDIA's Q1 release: the $91 billion Q2 guide assumes zero Data Center compute revenue from China. Read that twice. The largest infrastructure company in history just told the Street its forward number is good without the world's second-largest economy. And on the same weekend, Huawei announced its own "Tau Scaling Law" and LogicFolding architecture targeting 1.4nm-class silicon by 2031 without EUV. Two engineering empires, each pretending the other doesn't exist for modelling purposes. That is the decoupling moving from rhetoric into accounting.

The connecting thread runs through DeepSeek too, which made its 75% V4-Pro discount permanent on Friday — $0.87 per million output tokens, roughly a thirtieth of GPT-5.5. That isn't a discount, it's an efficiency curve being passed through. So when you stack the three together — NVIDIA's networking line at $14.8B/quarter (up 199%), Huawei's domestic stack push, and DeepSeek's price floor — what you're watching is the Chinese AI stack achieving what nuclear strategists used to call "second-source viability." The West and the East no longer share an infrastructure ramp. They share a benchmark leaderboard and nothing below it.

For India and Canada the implications diverge. In India, the take-up is simple: DeepSeek-class inference economics make sovereign-language model deployment ten times cheaper than it was twelve months ago, and the country's bigger play is selecting which stack to localise — including Cohere Apache 2.0, released yesterday. In Canada, the C-22 surveillance bill blew up over the weekend with Signal, NordVPN and ExpressVPN all publicly threatening to leave the country — at exactly the moment Carney is staking national strategy on Cohere's sovereign-AI thesis. You cannot run "Canada is the trusted AI jurisdiction" and "Canada wants encryption backdoors" simultaneously. One of them breaks. My read: C-22 gets gutted before second reading. The market has already priced that — TSX hit a record 34,831 yesterday on bank-earnings optimism, not on Ottawa policy clarity.

Watch oil. Trump said a deal is close, Iran said it isn't, WTI dropped to $91. We have now seen this exact movie seven times. The supply-side mechanics — depleted strategic stocks, Hormuz still closed, refiner inventories at multi-year lows — do not care about Trump's tweets. The whip will snap back.

⚡ SIGNAL SPOTLIGHT
NVIDIA's networking line ($14.8B last quarter, +199% YoY) is now bigger than the entire revenue of most top-10 chip companies — and that single line item, not the H200, is the AI-cluster choke point everyone has been mis-modelling. When the Street says "$91B guide," they mean "we don't know how to value the cables."
Compute scales linearly with GPU count. Networking scales super-linearly: doubling cluster size from 50K to 100K GPUs roughly triples the InfiniBand / NVLink / switch / NIC requirement. That is why Jensen's networking revenue grew 199% YoY while Data Center compute grew "only" 92%. Add Huawei's Tau Scaling Law announcement — explicitly aimed at moving data faster inside the chip rather than shrinking transistors — and you have both stacks converging on the same insight: the next 10x in AI is in interconnect economics, not transistor counts. Anyone modelling NVIDIA on a per-GPU TAM is using a 2024 framework on a 2027 business.
Time horizon: 6–18 months. Catalyst: Q2 FY27 earnings (late August 2026) tests whether networking growth holds above 150% YoY off the higher base.

SEMIS · CHINA ↑ Years horizon WATCH
Huawei's "Tau Scaling Law" is a manifesto, not a chip — but the LogicFolding architecture on this fall's Kirin Mate 90 is real silicon, and the 53.5% transistor-density jump matters

Huawei announced over the weekend a new "Tau Scaling Law" that proposes replacing Moore's geometric scaling with time-scaling (τ): instead of shrinking transistors, the framework optimises how fast data physically moves inside the chip. The paired LogicFolding architecture physically folds and stacks logic into dual layers. Huawei claims the 2026 Kirin chip (debuting in the Mate 90 this fall) will deliver a 53.5% transistor-density jump, 40% better P-core efficiency, and a peak clock of 3.1GHz — all without EUV lithography. The long-horizon target: 1.4nm-class chips by 2031. Innermost Loop framed this as "the substrate beneath all this is being rewritten just as fast." The same source notes that the AI race is now also a fab-architecture race, not just a model race.

SO WHAT   This is the first Chinese semiconductor disclosure that proposes a new physical paradigm rather than catching up to TSMC's existing one. Whether or not Huawei actually executes (history says: slip rate is high), the disclosure itself shifts the policy ground: the "China can't get past 7nm without EUV" frame is now publicly contested by Chinese engineers offering a different physics. Broad-operator implication: anyone selling into China-restricted markets needs to start tracking the alternative-substrate research line, not just lithography-node roadmaps.
ACTION   The first verifiable test is the Mate 90 launch this fall — if independent benchmarking confirms the 53.5% density claim on shipping silicon, the Tau Law thesis stops being a press release. That benchmark is the trigger event.
💭 The contrarian read: every Chinese fab announcement of the last decade has come with similar density claims that did not hold up to independent verification. Huawei's 2031 timeline is exactly the duration over which Western labs will refresh their fab tooling twice. The Tau Law is interesting as a research direction; as a competitive threat it is six years away and may never arrive.

Bill C-22 explodes: Signal, NordVPN and ExpressVPN all publicly threaten to leave Canada — and the US House Judiciary chair writes Ottawa warning the bill threatens cross-border data flows

BetaKit and Michael Geist's Sunday-Monday cycle confirmed what has become the most consequential Canadian tech-policy fight of the year. Bill C-22, the Lawful Access Act, would compel electronic service providers to build surveillance backdoors and retain user metadata for up to a year. Over the weekend: Signal's VP of strategy told the Globe and Mail it would pull out of Canada entirely rather than weaken end-to-end encryption; NordVPN issued a statement saying it would "consider all viable options, including limiting or, if necessary, removing our presence from Canadian jurisdiction"; ExpressVPN joined the same position; Windscribe said it would relocate HQ; Apple and Meta have publicly urged amendments. The chairs of the US House Judiciary and Foreign Affairs Committees wrote Public Safety Minister Anandasangaree warning the bill threatens US national security and cross-border data flows. Carney has not responded publicly.

SO WHAT   You cannot run "Canada is the trusted AI sovereign jurisdiction" (the Cohere/Carney pitch) and "Canada wants encryption backdoors" (the C-22 frame) at the same time. One of these has to break. The market has begun pricing the C-22 climbdown — Cohere's open-weights momentum and the Carney delegation's CEPA push to India both implicitly require the encryption story to hold. Broad-operator implication: the next 60 days will determine whether Canada's "trusted-stack" pitch survives intact or gets neutered.
ACTION   Anyone advising Canadian tech investments through year-end should be assigning probability weights to two outcomes: C-22 dies/gets gutted (positive for sovereign-AI thesis, probably 60-70%) or passes intact (kills the sovereignty pitch, exit risk rises across Cohere/Wealthsimple/Shopify/Lightspeed customer-trust premium).
💭 The contrarian read: Carney governments have shown willingness to die on hills more obscure than this (Online Streaming Act, Bill C-18). The Liberals may decide they need a "tough on tech" credential heading into 2027. If C-22 passes intact, the Signal/NordVPN exits actually become useful political theatre — "look at the foreign tech companies abandoning Canadian families." Don't assume the climbdown is automatic.

SO WHAT   The oil shock + INR weakness + driver-economics math is a four-way trigger for structural fleet electrification on a 6-12 month timeline, not the 3-5 year timeline most India EV theses still use.

The Long Game
