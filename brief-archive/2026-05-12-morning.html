Suhail Seth · May 12, 2026

Today the same gravitational object pulls every story on the page: compute. Anthropic just took the entire 300MW + 222,000-GPU capacity of SpaceX's Colossus 1 in Memphis. Cerebras bumped its IPO range to $150–$160 on a 20×-oversubscribed book and goes public Thursday at up to a $48.8B valuation. Oracle's $553B contracted backlog — 60% of it OpenAI — now nearly equals its market cap. Korea's Kospi is up 78% YTD because Samsung and SK Hynix together are 40% of the index, and Samsung crossed $1T market cap last week. Apple is reshoring some chip-making to Intel because TSMC capacity is gone. Kevin O'Leary's 9-gigawatt Stratos data center in Utah just drew 3,700 protest filings. Compute is now both the trade and the political flashpoint, simultaneously.

Both my countries are placing state-backed bets on the next substrate. Industry Minister Mélanie Joly announced Canada will privatize the Canadian Photonics Fabrication Centre — the only end-to-end compound-semiconductor foundry in North America. The on-stage line from CPFC's director general was that clients have asked to buy 100% of capacity. Joe Costello of Inpho compared it to early Silicon Valley: "There's not already some dominant player. You gotta come to Ottawa." India is taking a different bet: 12 fab projects in motion, two in commercial production, and IT Minister Vaishnaw confirmed Google is "seriously" considering manufacturing AI servers there. Canada is going long on photonics; India is going long on silicon. Same insight, different chemistry.

The connecting thread to the Iran war — now in its 11th week — is that the ceasefire has, in Trump's words, "a one per cent chance of living." WTI is back near $100. The Diet Coke shortage gripping India because aluminum can supply ran through the Persian Gulf is a tragicomic preview of how much of the modern compute supply chain is one Hormuz blockade away from collapse.

Forward call: by July 31, both India and Canada will have at least one ≥$100M compute-substrate equity event with a sovereign or pension anchor. CPFC will get its first signed term sheet within 12 weeks. Bet on it. (I'm logging both as predictions today.)

🇮🇳 INDIA · POLICY → By May 29 consultation close NOTE
SEBI brings open-market buybacks back — 66 working day window, MPS guardrails, merchant bankers optional, promoter ISIN-freeze

SEBI consultation paper (open until May 29) re-introduces stock-exchange-route buybacks that were phased out in 2018. New regime: 66 working days max window (PMAC asked for six months, SEBI overrode), 40% completion in first half, separate trading window scrapped (since the buyback distribution tax loophole that justified it is gone), promoter holdings frozen at ISIN level during the buyback period (depository-enforced, not just policy), explicit MPS protection (companies cannot announce buybacks that would breach Minimum Public Shareholding), merchant bankers no longer mandatory.

💭 Here is the version where everything above is wrong: PC Jeweller in 2018 is the cautionary tale SEBI conveniently sidelined.
