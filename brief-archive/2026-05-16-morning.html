GEO · MACRO▼72-HOUR HORIZONACT
Trump leaves Beijing with 200 Boeings and a soybean order — Wall Street loses 1%+ across the board because the trade desk had priced in Iran, Taiwan, and rare-earths

Trump and Xi closed their two-day Beijing summit on Friday. The deliverables: China commits to buy 200 Boeing jets, the US expects at least $10B in annual Chinese agricultural purchases, and the two sides agreed to set up a protocol to keep AI models out of non-state-actor hands. Trump said Xi agreed not to provide military equipment to Iran. The joint statement called it a “new vision of strategic stability” — the language Bishop’s Sinocism flagged as a Chinese framing victory two days ago. Critically: no binding Iran sanctions relief, no Taiwan-arms commitment from Trump (he said he was “noncommittal” on the $14B arms deal and would “decide soon”), no rare-earth export-license annex.

The market read was immediate and uniform. S&P 500 -1.24% to 7,408.50, Nasdaq -1.54% to 26,225.14, Dow -1.07% to 49,526.17 (lost the 50K mark it had just retaken Thursday). Nikkei -2.0%, Hang Seng -1.6%. The 10-year UST climbed to 4.58%, its highest yield since May 2025, on hot retail sales and the unresolved Hormuz oil premium. WTI +4.2% to ~$105. Xi cemented Taiwan as the top US-China risk by raising the Thucydides Trap framework directly with Trump — the bluntest language Xi has used on the topic. Bloomberg called it “underwhelming” in a headline.

SO WHAT: The pricing-in error is the durable lesson. Markets entered the week priced for two things the summit did not deliver: an Iran-sanctions de-escalation track and a rate-cut path supported by cooling US data. The 10Y print at 4.58% kills the second leg without a single chip-export announcement helping the first. Operator-grade reading: when both pillars of a bull tape crack on the same day, the next phase is positioning unwind, not new direction. The crowded long-tech book is the source of next week’s vulnerability. For India operators: a hawkish US duration regime keeps INR weak structurally. For Canada operators: a hawkish duration regime keeps the CAD bid against a low-volume currency that needs a BoC cut to stay competitive on the trade-exposed sector.
ACTION: Watch the 10Y for confirmation of 4.60%+ in the next 10 sessions. Watch the next CPI print and Fed minutes for the explicit Hormuz/energy-shock language. Watch Cerebras as the public-market avatar of the AI mark-up trade — if $240 cracks, the institutional flip is finally exhausted and price discovery begins.
💭 The contrarian read: this isn’t a regime change, it’s a healthy correction the market needed after three consecutive record-high closes. The Boeing order and agricultural purchases are real money that takes pressure off the next round of tariff threats. Xi’s Thucydides-Trap reference is the LEAST escalatory thing he could have said. The 10Y at 4.58% is hot data adjusting, not a regime break; one CPI miss and it’s back at 4.30%. The selloff is positioning, not fundamentals.

Carney and Smith sign the climate-and-energy agreement in Calgary — industrial carbon to $130/tonne by 2040, pipeline construction earliest September 2027 — but no BC-coast endpoint and no pipeline builder named

The federal-Alberta MoU landed in Calgary on Friday afternoon as promised. Alberta’s industrial emissions price will rise to $130/tonne by 2040, with a headline price of $140/tonne by 2040 — both materially below the prior national target of $170/tonne by 2030. In exchange, construction on a Pacific bitumen pipeline could begin as early as September 2027, dependent on the Pathways carbon-capture project advancing and on a mutual agreement with the Oil Sands Alliance consortium. Carney explicitly framed the agreement as “putting Canada on the path to net zero.”

What did NOT land: a specific BC-coast destination point (Prince Rupert / Kitimat / a named deepwater terminal). No named pipeline-builder consortium. No handoff to Ottawa’s Major Projects Office with a stated review date. The Trudeau-era $170-by-2030 escalator is legislatively dead; the political win for both leaders is real. The structural follow-through that would move Canadian critical-stack and pipeline-construction names from speculative to consensus is still pending.

SO WHAT: The MoU does what political agreements are supposed to do — it converts a contested policy battle into a project pipeline. But pricing the trade requires knowing the endpoint and the builder, and yesterday’s announcement deferred both. P175-2 resolves PARTIAL: the announcement happened today (the prediction’s primary requirement) but did not name the BC-coast point or the builder (the structural specificity). The next 14 days are the window for the route-and-builder reveal. If those land before May 30, the “Canada-as-AI-infrastructure-supplier” theme moves from active hypothesis to active trade. If they don’t, the trade stays in observation. For Indian operators: the message is that Canada is structurally separating from the US on energy-policy direction — the cross-border investment math has not been this favourable to Canadian resource and energy-infrastructure assets since 2014.
ACTION: 14-day watch window for the BC-coast destination and pipeline-builder reveal. If either lands by May 30, P176-1 triggers and the Canadian energy-to-AI-infra trade activates.
💭 The contrarian read: this is a deal that doesn’t survive its first First Nations consultation challenge. The 2027 construction date assumes a clean regulatory path that history says doesn’t exist on the BC coast. The $130/tonne softening might be the most concrete thing in the agreement; the pipeline itself is a 5-year political promise structured as a 12-week MoU.

INDIA · CONSUMER▲QUARTERS HORIZONWATCH
Inc42 frames the India D2C 3.0 moment — quick-commerce as the discovery-and-replenishment layer, premiumisation in F&B / wellness / beauty — and the mid-tier brands are the structural losers

Inc42’s Saturday weekly framed the India D2C 3.0 transition as decisive: the Meta-and-Shopify formula that built D2C 1.0 is over. Quick commerce is becoming the discovery and replenishment layer; marketplaces deliver scale; owned platforms deliver margin and retention. Premiumisation in F&B, beauty & personal care, wellness, supplements, and functional nutrition are the categories that compound (recurring purchase + premium price). Mid-tier brands are getting squeezed between hyper-premium and quick-commerce-discounted entrants.

SO WHAT: For Indian operators, the framework matters because the unit economics for D2C 3.0 are very different from 1.0. CAC has structurally risen; LTV is being driven by recurring premium SKUs, not viral one-shot purchases. Quick-commerce dependency adds platform risk. The winners are brands that can build offline trust + owned-platform retention + quick-commerce reach in parallel — not brands that pick one channel. The structural risk for foreign capital chasing the D2C 1.0 thesis is real and now embedded in valuations. For Indian capital deploying into D2C, the framework is now repeatable.
ACTION: Track the D2C raises in the next quarter. If F&B / wellness / personal-care brands are taking >60% of D2C funding, the 3.0 thesis is consensus. If general-merchandise D2C is back as a category, the cycle has reset to 1.0 and the framework is wrong.
💭 The contrarian: D2C 3.0 framing is a re-labelling of last year’s D2C 2.0 framing. The real disruption is quick-commerce eating the entire category — not a new D2C generation. Mid-tier brands aren’t losing to premium, they’re losing to Zepto / Blinkit / Instamart deciding to sell their own private labels.
